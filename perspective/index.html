Principles behind these suggestions

Composition guidelines are starting points. A centered arrangement, an asymmetric crop, or an expansive view supports different intentions. Empty-looking space must already exist in your subject. Review overlaps and edge crops yourself.

For a mirror still life, outline and select the mirror as your focus. Try the downward view, then check whether existing table edges and reflections lead toward it. The app does not identify mirrors or reconstruct their reflections.

Learn to see

Composition lexicon

Explore fundamentals, then look for them in film compositions. Each study connects a visual decision with a practical still-life exercise.

Fundamentals at a glance
Focal point
The area you intend viewers to notice first. Contrast, size and placement help establish it. View related study
Rule of thirds
Divide a frame into nine equal rectangles. Test a focal point near a grid intersection, then compare with centering. View related study
Balance
The distribution of visual weight across a frame. Small, dark shapes can balance larger, quieter areas. View related study
Symmetry
Related forms arranged around an axis. Centering a subject alone does not make its surroundings symmetrical. View related study
Leading lines
Visible edges or sequences that direct attention through a picture. Trace where they enter, turn and finish. View related study
Vanishing point
The projected meeting point of parallel lines sharing a spatial direction. It may lie far outside your canvas. View related study
Three-point perspective
Three perpendicular sets of scene edges converge toward three vanishing points. Tilting the view makes vertical convergence visible. View related study
Frame within a frame
An existing opening or contour encloses part of a composition, focusing attention and establishing spatial relationships. View related study
Figure–ground
How a subject separates from its surroundings through silhouette, value or color. Check its outline at a small scale. View related study
Negative space
The areas around and between objects. Their shapes contribute to composition as much as the objects themselves. View related study
Relative scale
The apparent size of one element compared with others or with the frame. It helps communicate distance and emphasis. View related study
Layering & overlap
Foreground, middle ground and background establish depth. Overlap helps show which forms sit in front of others. View related study
Deep focus
A photographic treatment that keeps near and distant subjects acceptably sharp within the same image. View related study
Foreshortening
A form appears shortened when it extends toward or away from the viewer. Compare its apparent dimensions from different viewpoints. View related study
High angle
A view directed downward at a subject. It changes visible top surfaces, overlaps and apparent proportions. View related study
Aspect ratio & crop
The frame’s width-to-height relationship and its chosen boundaries. They determine how much context surrounds your focus. View related study

Six film composition studies

Original schematic illustrations inspired by these films, not reproduced frames. Shapes and framing are simplified for study. Interpretations below are prompts for looking; source links provide film context and reference images where available.

01 / Balance

Symmetry & visual weight

A centered hotel study places matching architectural masses around a shared axis. Repetition gives the composition an ordered rhythm; a small figure becomes conspicuous within it.

Try in your still life Find a viewpoint where existing objects balance around your focus. Compare this centered arrangement with a thirds-based crop.

Heckmann, C. (2022, April 24). Wes Anderson symmetry & symmetrical editing explained. StudioBinder.

02 / Direction

Leading lines & convergence

The hotel corridor offers a clear perspective study. Ceiling, wall and floor edges converge into depth. Read those directions as a route for attention through the frame.

Try in your still life Use table edges or a mirror rim to lead toward your chosen focus. A vanishing point describes a direction; it need not coincide with your subject.

Brown, G. (2022, March 1). The Steadicam and The Shining revisited. American Cinematographer.

03 / Emphasis

Frame within a frame

In the closing doorway composition, dark interior edges surround a bright exterior and an isolated figure. The inner frame separates belonging from exclusion.

Try in your still life Look through an existing opening, handle or mirror frame. Change your viewpoint until its contour supports the focal object without obscuring it.

British Film Institute. (n.d.). The Searchers (1956).

04 / Scale

Negative space & relative size

Sherif Ali first appears as a distant speck in the desert. A tiny figure surrounded by broad, quiet areas makes distance and anticipation part of the image.

Try in your still life Leave a quieter region of table or background around one object. Compare its size with the whole canvas and notice whether it remains legible.

Houston, P. (2017, September 29). Lawrence of Arabia archive review: The hazy draw of the desert. Sight and Sound.

05 / Relationships

Layering & deep focus

Young Charles remains visible outside while adults decide his future indoors. Foreground, middle ground and background hold related actions within one composition.

Try in your still life Use viewpoint to separate overlapping objects across depth. Keep a visible path toward your focus. Deep focus describes optical sharpness, not three-point perspective.

Singer, L. (2016, April 29). 20 inspired visual moments in Citizen Kane. British Film Institute.

06 / Viewpoint

High angle & diagonal movement

The downward stairwell view makes receding shapes and converging edges prominent. The film also uses a dolly zoom to alter perceived depth; this diagram isolates its spatial composition.

Try in your still life Raise your viewpoint and inspect foreshortened surfaces. For a mirror still life, compare whether descending edges strengthen the intended sense of looking inward.

American Film Institute. (n.d.). Vertigo (1958). AFI Catalog.

From photograph to drawing

Create drawing guide traces visible contrast edges after smoothing texture. It preserves image proportions and does not treat patches of color as objects. Use Outline objects to mark meaningful silhouettes or fit boxes, cylinders and ellipses. Switch to Original photo at any time to compare.

One photograph cannot supply exact hidden geometry or real-world measurements. Photo and contour layers are flat references; outlined forms use an editable depth estimate. Viewpoint previews are shallow studies, not reconstructed views of hidden surfaces or reflections. Small changes are most useful.

Freeze locks the current canvas crop, proportions and line settings. Print produces a page-sized guide labeled with your intended canvas dimensions. For transfer, use the same width-to-height ratio. Printing is fitted to paper rather than full-size tiled output.
