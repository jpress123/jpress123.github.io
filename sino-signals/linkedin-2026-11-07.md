What should a surgeon learn to feel through a robot?

At Shanghai’s import expo, Intuitive Fosun showed the da Vinci 5 in China for the first time. Its force feedback gives the surgeon another cue alongside the image on the console. The company also introduced a locally developed digital platform.

The part hospital leaders might overlook is the training network: Intuitive Fosun reports 28 da Vinci training centres. That creates an opportunity to teach a difficult judgment. When the image looks reassuring but the instrument feels wrong, what should the operator do? The network count does not tell us how many clinicians have trained on this model or whether patients fare better.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take for Western hospitals to write that exercise before signing the purchase order? If the supplier alone defines what good performance feels like, who decides when a surgeon is ready, and whose experience gets left out?
