What happens when a platform decides that two AI characters looking alike is a defect it can measure?

On August 3, Hongguo, ByteDance's free short-drama platform, switched on an AI facial-similarity comparison system. Inside a single series, leads and core supporting characters must carry distinct features, expressions, and posture. Swapping only hairstyle and costume over the same underlying face template is a violation. Across series, any character scoring above 65 percent facial similarity to an existing one is rejected outright. Urban romance, family warmth, and urban comeback titles fall under the standard. Fantasy, ancient-style, and hand-drawn manhua dramas keep the old rules. A self-correction window ran July 22 to August 2.

Why it scales in China: Hongguo and Douyin sit between tens of thousands of studios and the audience, and AI titles already account for more than 95 percent of new micro-drama releases. The rules are benchmarked to the National Radio and Television Administration's grading and provenance requirements, so platform enforcement and state policy point the same way. Urban titles must lip-sync dubbing, vary camera language, label opening credits as AI-assisted production, and embed provenance metadata in the file. Penalties escalate from rejection to takedown, revenue clawback, and account bans.

Why Western leaders should care: this is the first platform rule anywhere that puts a number on generative sameness. It also turns a face into a registered asset. Training characters on unauthorized real-person photos, celebrity likenesses, or original illustrations is banned. Custom virtual likenesses need written authorization on file. YouTube, Netflix, ReelShort, and DramaBox face the same flood with no comparable instrument.

Monday morning: ask whoever runs your generated-content pipeline what the reuse rate is on faces, voices, and character designs, and whether you can measure it at all. Build the provenance record now. Assume a similarity threshold lands in your market within eighteen months, and that the audit trail you lack today becomes the cost you pay then.
