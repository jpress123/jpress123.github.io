## China Rewrites Vehicle Size Rules to Make Room for Self-Driving Sensors

China's State Administration for Market Regulation approved mandatory national standard GB 1589-2026 and announced it on August 31, 2026. The standard governs exterior dimensions, axle loads, and masses for motor vehicles, trailers, and combination vehicles, and the revision carves out protrusion exemptions for radar, cameras, and V2X antennas covering blind-spot monitoring, autonomous-driving perception, vehicle-road coordination, and beyond-line-of-sight safety communication. These are targeted exemptions for smart-vehicle hardware, not a blanket increase in length and width limits. The standard also raises the maximum permissible load for single axles with single tires on each side from 7,000 to 8,000 kilograms, relieving the front-axle overloading caused by concentrated battery mass on commercial electric vehicles. New type approvals comply from July 1, 2027, and existing approvals from January 1, 2028.

One regulator sets one mandatory standard on one published date, so the whole supply chain retools against a single deadline. Chinese suppliers already manufacture the radar, cameras, and V2X modules the exemptions were written for, and they now have a sanctioned envelope to design into. The staged transition through January 2028 lets current models sell through while new platforms get drawn around the sensors from the first sketch.

Regulation usually lags autonomy hardware. Western engineers have spent years packaging sensors inside dimension rules written for mirrors and bumpers, accepting compromised placement and reduced coverage as the cost of compliance. Beijing moved the rules instead, which lowers the cost of perception coverage on every vehicle built to Chinese type approval. Foreign automakers selling into China face the same 2027 date, and vehicles engineered to the Chinese envelope will reach export markets carrying sensor placements their home regulators have yet to sanction.

Have your homologation team map GB 1589-2026 against your 2027 and 2028 China product plans this week, and flag every sensor placement still constrained by the old envelope. Then ask regulatory affairs what filing for equivalent exemptions at home would require.

## Tencent Takes Its In-House Game AI Pipeline to Gamescom and Global Studios

Tencent Games Central Tech made its gamescom debut as a unified brand in Cologne on August 27, 2026, presenting six proprietary systems: Motus AI for character animation, GIGA for game agents, MagicDawn for rendering and global illumination, Anti-Cheat Expert for security, GVoice for voice communication, and WeTest for automated testing. Motus runs on Tencent's own family of generative animation foundation models and joins rigging, intelligent skinning, text-, video- and keyframe-driven motion generation, motion refinement, and real-time character interaction into one continuous pipeline. Tencent is precise about why the pipeline matters more than the generator: characters must be rigged and skinned before generation helps, and generated motion needs correction for jitter, mesh penetration, joint anomalies, and foot sliding, so fragmented tools give back in cleanup what AI saves in creation. On August 24 at gamescom dev, Motus technical lead Zijiao Zeng presented the pipeline to Western studio engineers.

Tencent runs games at a scale where animation cleanup is a line item measured in artist-years, so a pipeline that removes those artifacts pays for itself inside a single title. The models train on motion data from Tencent's own live portfolio, a corpus no Western middleware vendor can assemble, and Chinese studios staff larger in-house technology teams than most Western publishers, which shortens the distance from research model to production tool.

Western studios buy animation tooling from Autodesk, Epic, Unity, and a fragmented middleware layer, then absorb the integration cost between them. Tencent is offering the integrated version, proven on shipping titles, to the studios that compete with it. Selling the toolchain instead of the game moves leverage upstream, and every studio that adopts Motus or GIGA gives Tencent visibility into how the next generation of games gets made.

Ask your production leads which parts of the creative pipeline you stitch together from three vendors, and price what an integrated alternative would save.

## The Common Move

Both signals show a Chinese institution operating one layer below where competitors are working. A Western automaker optimizes sensor placement within fixed rules; SAMR changed the rules. A Western studio optimizes its animation workflow across vendor tools; Tencent built the pipeline and now sells it. Advantage at the substrate layer is slower to build and much harder to answer, because responding requires changing a standard or replacing a toolchain rather than shipping a better product. Watch which Western regulators and platform owners start working at that layer, and how quickly.
