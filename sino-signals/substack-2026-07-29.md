Sino-Signals | July 29, 2026 — China Scales AI Video and Humanoid Robots

*Kuaishou's Kling raises three billion dollars for its video model while Beijing projects one hundred thousand humanoid robots this year.*

Two signals today mark the same shift, Chinese AI moving from demonstration to industrial supply. One turns generative video into a funded global platform. The other turns humanoid robots into a production forecast with a hard number attached.

### Kuaishou's Kling AI Raises Three Billion to Scale Its Video Model Worldwide

On July 2, 2026, Kuaishou disclosed that its Kling AI video generation unit is raising up to 20.4 billion yuan, about 3 billion dollars, at a post-money valuation near 18 billion. The round drew 38 investors, including Alibaba Cloud, Tencent, Baidu, CITIC Securities, Shanghai state capital, and Abu Dhabi's BlueFive, and it cuts Kuaishou's stake from full ownership to about 68 percent. Kling reports more than 100 million registered users across 224 countries, roughly 50,000 enterprise clients, and over 600 million videos generated since its June 2024 launch. First quarter revenue passed 650 million yuan, up more than 300 percent year on year.

Kling draws on Kuaishou's 410 million daily users and a 1.6 trillion yuan e-commerce base, which supply advertising, short drama, and product use cases that convert generation into paid demand. Annualized recurring revenue reached about 500 million dollars by March, and state funds, banks, and a listed film studio invested together behind one national champion.

A Chinese model raising the biggest round in AI video resets the benchmark that Sora, Veo, and Runway answer to. Kling already earns from paying users worldwide, and Kuaishou plans a Hong Kong listing within twelve months. Studios and platforms everywhere now compare a cheaper, faster pipeline against incumbent tools.

### China Projects Its Humanoid Robot Output Will Top 100,000 Units This Year

On July 7, 2026, Ministry of Industry and Information Technology official Gan Xiaobang told a World AI Conference briefing in Shanghai that China's full-year humanoid robot output should top 100,000 units this year. He noted that above-scale industrial firms already exceed 30 percent AI adoption, supported by a national field-training program for embodied intelligence. The forecast follows UBTech logging more than 13,361 orders for its UWORLD U1 line at a June 30 launch and Unitree clearing a fast-tracked Shanghai IPO with a 20,000-unit shipping target.

China deploys humanoid units into its own auto plants first, with UBTech Walker S2 robots working lines at BYD, Geely, FAW-Volkswagen, and Foxconn. Domestic shipments rose from about 18,000 in 2025 toward a projected 62,500 in 2026, and a dense supply chain in motors, actuators, and batteries pushes unit prices below 120,000 yuan.

A hundred thousand units in one year would move the category from demonstration to industrial supply, ahead of Tesla's Optimus timeline. Western manufacturers facing labor shortages will meet Chinese robots with a real factory record and a falling price curve.
