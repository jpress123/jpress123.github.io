How long does it take your utility to plan a piece of the grid? In Shanghai, an AI just did it in 10 minutes.

At the 2026 World AI Conference, China Southern Power Grid demonstrated MegaWatt Yunrui, an AI-native agent that produced a complete distribution network plan, covering risk diagnosis, grid layout, simulation checks, and an investment report, in 10 minutes. Judges named it one of the show's top 10 exhibits, the only entry from a central state-owned enterprise, chosen from more than 3,000 products across 1,100 companies. The agent runs on a purpose-built power model and sits inside China's first electricity-carbon-computing platform, already live across five provinces.

Why it scales in China: the same platform compresses carbon measurement from monthly to minute-level and steers computing jobs toward the hours and places with cheap green power. China is adding AI compute faster than its grid can absorb, so software that plans networks and matches data-center demand to clean supply answers a national constraint. Coordination between computing and power entered China's Government Work Report for the first time this year, which gives the work central backing and a fast path across provincial grids.

For Western leaders: your utilities face the same surge in AI electricity demand, while grid planning still takes weeks and carbon accounting lags a month behind. An agent that plans in minutes and prices carbon by the minute resets the speed and transparency baseline you will be measured against. Southern Power Grid is now building overseas demonstration projects, exporting the design.

Monday morning: time how long your own grid connection and network planning take, then benchmark against a 10-minute baseline and ask vendors for minute-level carbon accounting. Treat electricity-carbon-computing coordination as a procurement question this quarter, not a research topic.
