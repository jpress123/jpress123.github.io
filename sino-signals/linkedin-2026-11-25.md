When every image can be generated, where does the director’s responsibility begin?

Shenzhen’s approximately 90-minute film 奇妙连接 launched on Shenzhen TV, Hunan TV and Mango TV on November 23. China News Service reports that its imagery was generated without conventional location filming.

The production still required human decisions about story, characters, composition and lighting. Location research informed digital city settings. The organisational change is a production team directing generated assets through a complete feature, with established broadcasters providing distribution.

For Western film leaders, this raises practical questions about commissioning and accountability. A convincing shot does not explain who checked its provenance, continuity or permissions. The report offers no independent cost comparison or audience results, so commercial advantage remains unproven.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take to commission a generated feature with clear creative ownership? Which approval and rights practices must change before production accelerates? If leaders leave those decisions to tool vendors, who sets the creative agenda, and which filmmakers retain agency or risk exclusion?
