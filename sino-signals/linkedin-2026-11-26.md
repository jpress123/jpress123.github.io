When AI agents can generate a trading strategy, who takes responsibility for implementing it?

Postal Savings Bank’s U-Quant.AI brings coding and backtesting agents into its interbank trading workflow. China Post News reported the deployment on November 23, describing a system connecting contract searches, market queries, strategy generation, simulation and trading.

The organisational choice, and challenge, is integration. Specialist agents work inside an existing financial markets system, while established research methods become shared institutional knowledge. For the Chinese bank, this creates a route for making individual expertise reusable across teams.

For Western banking leaders, integration means connecting research, technology, risk and trading around clear responsibility. Who validates an agent’s proposal, authorises execution and intervenes when conditions change? The corporate report provides no independent risk-adjusted performance results or detail on approval controls.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take to integrate those responsibilities as deliberately as the technology? Which departmental boundaries and incentives must change? If leaders connect the tools but leave accountability fragmented, who decides what reaches the market, and who retains the authority to stop it?
