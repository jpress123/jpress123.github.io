What happens to your AI cost model when one of the cheapest inference providers in the world stops buying American chips to run its models?

DeepSeek plans to install at least 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT accelerators at a gigawatt-scale data center in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, about 350 kilometers northwest of Beijing, Bloomberg reported on September 4. Part of the site is scheduled to come online by late 2027 or early 2028. The split matters: inference moves to Huawei silicon, training stays on Nvidia. DeepSeek raised more than 7 billion dollars in June 2026 to stop renting compute and start owning it.

Why China can do this: Ulanqab has cheap wind and solar and a 4.3 degree Celsius average temperature, so free cooling and stranded renewable power make a gigawatt site pencil out. State procurement rules already require Chinese chips in state-funded data centers, giving Huawei guaranteed demand while it scales an untested domestic high-bandwidth memory supply chain. Huawei launches the 950DT in Q4 2026, and memory shortages hold output to the low hundreds of thousands of units this year.

Why Western leaders should care: splitting training and inference across two silicon vendors is the workaround every export-controlled buyer will copy. It underwrites Huawei's accelerator business, tracking from 7.5 billion dollars in 2025 toward roughly 12 billion in 2026. And cheap domestic inference is exactly what lets Chinese open-weight models keep undercutting Western API pricing, which is where this reaches your budget.

Monday morning: ask your infrastructure team what you pay per million inference tokens, and how that compares with Chinese open-weight models served on domestic silicon. Then model what a second credible inference vendor does to your Nvidia negotiating position.
