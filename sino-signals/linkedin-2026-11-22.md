Who gets to decide whether a hospital AI tool is ready for the next ward?

Beijing’s national medical AI pilot base brings hospitals and developers together around data governance, annotation and clinical validation. A November 18 report says a brain CT assistant developed with Tiantan Hospital has reached more than 40 medical institutions. That deployment count alone does not establish patient benefit.

The organisational choice matters. Shared services give participating hospitals a route from clinical requirements to testing and wider use. For Western hospital networks, the question is whether their own validation resources can travel across institutions while retaining local clinical scrutiny.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take for Western leaders to fund shared testing and give clinicians authority to challenge results? Which barriers to data access and collaboration must change? If hospitals leave those choices to suppliers, who sets the acceptance criteria, and which smaller institutions lose the ability to make an informed decision?
