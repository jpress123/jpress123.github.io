## Fudan Stores a Data Bit in One Electron

A Fudan University team led by Liu Chunsen and Zhou Peng built a two-dimensional quantum flash memory that stores one bit by trapping and reading a single electron at room temperature, published in Science on July 17, 2026. The device holds its charge with no power and opens a 0.5 volt memory window from that lone electron, a tenfold gain over earlier single-electron work. Today's DRAM needs about 200,000 electrons to hold one bit. The result follows the group's PoX flash in Nature in April 2025 and its CY-01 two-dimensional flash chip in October 2025.

The work comes from Fudan's State Key Laboratory of Integrated Chips and Systems after more than a decade of device physics, the sort of long-horizon bet that state key labs fund through slow cycles. China wants to own its memory and chip stack, so a domestic result at the storage limit fits national compute and self-reliance goals.

Memory that is fast, low power, and non-volatile at the single-electron limit would collapse the tradeoff between today's DRAM and NAND and cut the energy AI systems spend holding data. A design that reaches a fab resets the density and power math that Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron now set. Production remains years away, and the team also reported a new quantum effect it calls a density of states scissors that could speed the engineering.

Monday morning: treat memory energy, not only compute, as a first-order AI cost, and track Fudan's lab to fab timeline and its two-dimensional memory patents.

## Insilico's AI-Designed Drug Reaches Phase III

Insilico Medicine registered a Phase III trial of Rentosertib in July 2026, the first medicine with both an AI-found target and an AI-designed molecule to reach that stage. The oral drug treats idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and will enroll 320 patients across 47 centers in China over 52 weeks against placebo, with enrollment set for August 2026. The Hong Kong-listed company says its fastest program reached candidate nomination in nine months, against about four and a half years for conventional work. It holds 31 preclinical candidates and 13 investigational new drug clearances since 2021.

Insilico runs its AI research in Montreal and Abu Dhabi and does validation, screening, and scale-up in Shanghai, where CEO Alex Zhavoronkov credits local infrastructure, cost, and a 2025 30-working-day trial review pathway with removing about two years from a program. A Pfizer executive puts clinical development in China at three times faster and half the cost of Europe.

Chinese-linked biotechs took 6 of 26 major pharma licensing and acquisition deals over 16 months, worth about 53 billion dollars, with Lilly, Takeda, Merck, Sanofi, AstraZeneca, and AbbVie all sourcing assets from the country. An AI-designed drug in Phase III pressures Western biotech on cost and novelty at once. Whether AI molecules win more often in late-stage trials stays unproven for now.

Monday morning: benchmark discovery to candidate timelines against a nine to thirteen month AI baseline, and decide which assets to license from Chinese AI-biotech now versus build in-house.
