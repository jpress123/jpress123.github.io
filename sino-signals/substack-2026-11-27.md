# Sino-Signals | November 27, 2026

## Integration needs an owner

*Two institutional approaches to turning AI access into accountable work.*

Today’s entries examine who maintains an AI service after its introduction. One concerns education research; the other examines retail adoption. Their shared relevance is organisational, but their evidence differs: a launch announcement establishes a pilot, while a survey records respondents’ reported experience. Neither should be read as a controlled evaluation of impact.

## China’s Education Research Institute Launches a Specialist AI Agent

On November 15, the Chinese National Academy of Educational Sciences introduced Jiaozhi No. 1 and announced initial pilot partners. Its November 16 report describes a specialist education-research agent using retrieval-augmented generation and education research data, operated by the academy’s audiovisual publishing house.

The organisational design brings a research institution, a publishing operator and pilot organisations into one programme. This creates a route for testing shared research support, rather than leaving every teacher to assemble separate tools.

For education leaders elsewhere, the useful comparison is who curates evidence and owns the service. The launch report does not establish independent accuracy, classroom outcomes or widespread adoption.

The practical question for a school or university is whether the service strengthens its own capacity to assess evidence. A research assistant can present a plausible synthesis while leaving uncertainty about missing studies, conflicting findings or the suitability of a recommendation for a particular classroom. A responsible institutional design would make those uncertainties discussable. That is an editorial implication, not a demonstrated feature of the announced product. Before procurement, leaders should ask for a worked example showing how an unsupported answer is challenged and corrected, and who maintains that correction over time.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take to connect research support with accountable editorial review? If institutions leave evidence selection to vendors, who sets the questions teachers can ask, and who retains authority to challenge the answers?

Source: [Chinese National Academy of Educational Sciences](https://www.cnaes.edu.cn/post/151972)

## China’s Retail Survey Exposes the Gap Between AI Deployment and Scale

Deloitte China’s November 25 summary of its joint CCFA retail study reports that more than half of surveyed enterprises have deployed agents or use public generative AI applications, while more than eight in ten have not reached scale. The research draws on association members and partners, not a representative census of Chinese retail.

An industry association provides a channel for retailers to compare practices across firms. The report separates technical feasibility, real pilots and scaled operation, with permissions matched to risk.

Western retailers should test whether a successful demonstration can survive different stores, staff and data conditions. Survey responses and consultant recommendations do not prove revenue gains or independently audited productivity improvements.

For a retailer, a demonstration in one setting is a starting point for questions about other settings. Different store formats can have different staffing, stock and service constraints. Leaders need to distinguish a proposal for wider deployment from evidence that it works across those differences. An operational owner should be able to explain what happens when the model recommends an action that the store cannot perform. This is our interpretation of the management challenge, not a claim that the surveyed firms have already solved it.

Thinking futureback about this signal, who would own the work of making an AI process repeatable across stores? Which incentives and decision rights must change, and whose agency is lost if deployment expands before staff can contest its decisions?

Source: [Deloitte China and CCFA](https://www.deloitte.com/cn/zh/Industries/consumer/perspectives/ccfa-gai-white-paper-2026.html)

The connecting question is where authority sits when an AI service becomes part of everyday work. Shared infrastructure can make access easier, but access alone does not determine who checks evidence, adapts a workflow or handles an exception.
