What happens to your content strategy when a streaming platform can extend a franchise without assembling a production? China just answered that question in public, twice in one month.

On August 21, iQIYI and Changxin Media confirmed the Soul Ferryman "Fusheng Meng" trilogy. The first two films, Butterfly Dream and Celestial Maiden Dream, went live at 10am on August 22, exclusive to iQIYI. Each runs over an hour. Each was made with generative AI. Guo Jingyu served as art director, Ding Kuan directed, Li Zhichao produced. To rebuild the franchise's original trio at a younger age, the production licensed the actors' likenesses, brought back original cast members Yu Yi, Liu Zhiyang, and Xiao Yin to record live voice performances, and used motion transfer to drive the characters on screen. This is iQIYI's second AIGC feature release inside four weeks.

Why it scales in China: iQIYI owns both ends. It green-lights against its own subscriber data and distributes to hundreds of millions of accounts on day one. The Soul Ferryman universe already spans long-form drama, web film, a musical, and short drama, so the audience and the rights position are already in place. Regulators opened a licensing route for AI-generated features this summer, which means compliance is settled before the first frame.

Why Western leaders should care: a major platform is now shipping feature-length AI content at franchise cadence, roughly one release every two weeks, behind a paywall. The likeness plus voice plus motion transfer arrangement is a working answer to the question Hollywood is still litigating. The cost floor for catalog extension is moving.

Monday morning: identify the IP in your catalog that has no economic case for a new production, then price what an AI-assisted extension would cost against the rights you would need to clear. Check whether your talent contracts treat likeness, voice, and motion capture as separable.
