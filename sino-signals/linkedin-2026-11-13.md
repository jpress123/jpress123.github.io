When a studio can dub a feature in six days, who gets the last word on how an actor sounds in another language?

China Film’s AI research institute used its own dubbing model to make the English version of Pegasus 3. Its director, Ma Ping, says the work took six days and the model reproduced characteristics of the actors’ voices. He was describing a film already released in Chinese and English.

For a distributor, the interesting decision comes before opening night. If the English track can be ready alongside the original, a director can review both performances while there is still time to change a line. Six days tells us how quickly one production finished. It tells us little about how a joke landed or who could ask for a second take.

Thinking futureback about this signal, would your studio give an actor the right to reject a synthetic reading in a language they don’t speak? Who would help them hear what changed, and whose deadline wins if they object?
