What happens to Western pharma economics when an AI-designed drug clears the lab on a China-speed clock?

Insilico Medicine has registered a Phase III trial of Rentosertib, the first medicine with both an AI-found target and an AI-designed molecule to reach that stage. The oral treatment for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis will enroll 320 patients across 47 centers in China over 52 weeks, with enrollment set to begin in August 2026. Insilico says its fastest program reached candidate nomination in nine months, against about four and a half years for conventional work, and it now holds 31 preclinical candidates and 13 IND clearances since 2021.

Why this matters in China: Insilico validates and scales in Shanghai, where CEO Alex Zhavoronkov credits local infrastructure, lower cost, and a 2025 30-working-day trial review pathway with removing about two years from a program. A Pfizer executive puts clinical development in China at three times faster and half the cost of Europe. In 2025, more drugs made their first market debut in China than in any other country, the first time it passed the United States.

Global impact: Chinese-linked biotechs took 6 of 26 major pharma deals over 16 months, worth about 53 billion dollars, with Lilly, Takeda, Merck, Sanofi, AstraZeneca, and AbbVie all sourcing assets from the country. An AI-designed drug in Phase III pressures Western biotech on cost and novelty at the same time. Whether AI molecules win more often in late-stage trials stays unproven.
