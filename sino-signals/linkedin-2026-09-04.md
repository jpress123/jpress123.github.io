What does it actually cost a Western platform to sell AI inside China? Apple just published the invoice.

Reuters reported on August 14 that Apple trained its own large language model for the China market with help from Alibaba, abandoning the plan to simply license a domestic model. The Cyberspace Administration of China logged the service in July, making Apple the first foreign company approved to run a proprietary AI model on the mainland. That clearance arrived roughly 22 months after Apple Intelligence shipped everywhere else. Alibaba's Qwen and Baidu technology sit alongside the new model in a dual-track deployment, with rollout expected alongside iOS 27.

Why this works in China: regulatory approval, not compute, is the scarce asset. Alibaba supplies the Chinese-language training corpus, the compliant filtering stack, and mainland inference capacity that no foreign firm can assemble alone under current data rules. Apple got in by co-developing on Chinese infrastructure instead of importing a finished product.

Why it matters everywhere else: this is now the template. Train locally, partner with a licensed domestic lab, submit to filing, and accept a China-specific model that diverges from your global one. Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI hold no equivalent clearance. Apple gains a structural advantage in the world's largest smartphone market and accepts a permanently bifurcated product to get it.

Monday morning: map which of your AI features would require a separately filed model in China and identify who your domestic training partner would be. Price two model stacks against the revenue at risk. Regulatory clearance is a product requirement with a lead time, not a legal afterthought.
