Sino-Signals | Thursday, August 20, 2026 — China Plans the Power and the Robot on the Same Clock

Beijing commits its grid to 50 percent non-fossil generation by 2030, and its flagship humanoid lab points its next machine at the living room.

Two signals today, one about the floor and one about the room above it. The first is a five-year construction plan that treats computing demand as something to plan for. The second is a consumer humanoid unveiled where the whole supply chain now fits under one roof.

## China Orders Its Grid to Reach 50 Percent Non-Fossil Power by 2030

The National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration issued the 15th Five-Year Plan for New Power System Construction, document 942, signed July 21 and released August 3, 2026. By 2030 non-fossil sources must supply 50 percent of generation, the grid must absorb more than 2.8 billion kilowatts of renewables at high utilization, and the charging network must support over 110 million electric vehicles. Conventional hydropower reaches roughly 410 million kilowatts, nuclear about 110 million kilowatts, and biomass, solar thermal, geothermal and marine generation together about 65 million kilowatts. Coordinated development of electricity and computing power appears in the list of named priority tasks.

One document binds every provincial energy bureau, both grid operators, and the state generators to the same numbers, so planning, capital, and procurement move together. It reaches down to the meter, requiring faster grid-connection service for residential charging points and pilots for unified construction and operation of charging facilities in housing estates. Transport and energy convergence is its own work item, siting wind, solar, storage, charging and battery-swap stations along expressways and sharing corridors, ducts and pylons between the two networks.

The global implication sits in the sequencing. China is treating data-center load as a planning input to the power system, while most Western grids are absorbing AI demand as an unplanned shock with interconnection queues of five to seven years. If the compute and electricity build-outs stay coupled, Chinese AI capacity carries a lower and more predictable marginal power cost. The EV charging target becomes the reference point European and North American charging economics get measured against through 2030.

Monday morning action: ask what your next compute site will pay for delivered power in 2030 and how long it waits for interconnection, and treat a vague answer as an unpriced risk on the AI roadmap.

## Beijing's Humanoid Lab Launches a Home Robot for the Mass Market

The 2026 World Robot Conference opened August 19 in Beijing E-Town, its permanent venue, across roughly 50,000 square meters with more than 300 exhibitors, over 2,000 exhibits, and about 150 product debuts. The Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center unveiled Tiangong Omni, a small humanoid built for the consumer home market, alongside the embodied multimodal model Pelican-Unify 1.0. Tiangong Omni opens its joint control, sensor streams, motion control and data-collection interfaces to outside developers, with household chores, companionship for the old and the young, and home security named as target tasks. In JD's 600 square meter zone, 45 industry products debuted, and Honor, Hisense and TCL each showed a robot product for the first time.

China now has the full stack in one hall. Galaxea demonstrated robots taking live orders inside a working JD Logistics forward warehouse, handling picking, navigation, packing and placement with its G0.5 embodied model and few-shot SKU recognition instead of per-item programming. The consumer electronics brands matter more than the demos, because they bring distribution, service networks, and the manufacturing discipline that moves a device from thousands of units to millions.

The center of gravity is shifting from industrial pilots to household products, and an open developer interface invites the third-party ecosystem that made Chinese drones and electric vehicles hard to compete with on price. Tesla, Figure and 1X are building capable machines aimed at labor markets. Beijing is aiming at the living room, where volume and interaction data accumulate faster, and the better home model follows the data.

Monday morning action: if your company sells anything that lives in a home, name the person who owns the question of what changes when a humanoid is in that room, and watch the appliance brands rather than the robot startups for the retail crossover.

Both signals show the same discipline at different layers. China decides the constraint early, writes a number against it, and lets industry build toward the number. Electricity and compute get planned as one system, and the household robot gets an open interface before it has a market. Neither decision guarantees the outcome, and both make it cheaper to reach than it will be for anyone deciding these questions after the demand arrives.
