If a ward questions an IV dose, can the pharmacist reconstruct how it was prepared?

In Beijing, Meilan’s North China Innovation Center demonstrated a robot that takes a medication order through labelling, vial disinfection, mixing, checking and waste disposal. The system records the preparation on video, giving the pharmacist a record to consult alongside the order.

That matters in hospital pharmacies where staff repeatedly handle hazardous drugs. Moving some of that work into a machine could free pharmacists for prescription review and patient counselling. The demonstration leaves an important question open for hospitals elsewhere. How reliably can staff spot and resolve an exception during a busy shift?

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would it take for Western hospital leaders to give pharmacists the time and authority to stop a questionable dose? If the vendor decides which exceptions become visible, what evidence will a patient or a ward nurse have when they challenge a preparation?
