How much does a million tokens cost you once you count the electricity and the carbon? China now asks that question at the national planning level, and has the numbers to answer it.

At the 2026 Green Computing (AI) Conference in Hohhot on August 22, the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology and the National Data Administration put the figures on the record. China's computing facilities burned 170 billion kWh of electricity in 2025, up about 30 percent year on year. More than 160 data centers now hold 4A or higher green ratings, average PUE at hyperscale sites has fallen to 1.34, and 42 clusters of 10,000 accelerators or more are running. Hohhot and Ulanqab signed 13 projects worth 136.1 billion yuan the same week, covering chip testing, equipment manufacturing, compute centers, and token factories.

Why it scales in China: compute-electricity coordination, suan dian xie tong, entered the government work report for the first time this year. That gives provinces a mandate to put compute where clean power already sits. Hohhot reports 7.12 million kW of installed new energy capacity, green electricity above 85 percent at its data centers, and power at roughly 0.35 yuan per kWh. The National Energy Administration expects compute electricity demand to grow by more than 100 billion kWh a year through the 15th Five-Year Plan, so the siting decision gets made once, centrally, for a decade.

Why Western leaders should care: the competition is moving from cluster size to delivered cost per token under a carbon constraint. Daily token calls in China reached about 140 trillion by March 2026, up from roughly 100 billion in early 2024. Hyperscalers waiting years in grid interconnection queues face a rival whose siting, tariffs, green certificate trading, and carbon accounting all move through one planning cycle.

Monday morning: ask your infrastructure team for cost per million tokens including electricity and carbon, not GPU hours. Then ask where the power for your next training run comes from, when it connects, and who is queued ahead of you.
