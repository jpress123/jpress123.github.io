Sino-Signals | Friday, October 2, 2026, A $6,900 Language Model and a Cluster Service Short of Capacity

China's universities train on gaming GPUs while Huawei Cloud sells 100,000-card clusters it cannot yet supply abroad.

Two signals today sit at opposite ends of the compute spectrum. One shows a university lab pretraining a model on hardware sold to gamers, and the other shows a vendor selling the largest clusters China can build. Both rest on single reported sources, and each entry below flags what remains unverified.

## Tsinghua Trains a Competitive Open LLM on Gaming GPUs for About $6,900

**What happened:** A Tsinghua University and Pengcheng Laboratory team posted PuRo-2B, a 2-billion-parameter model pretrained from scratch on consumer RTX 5090 GPUs. The paper reports about $6,900 to reproduce the canonical model, 1.4 trillion training tokens in two phases, and 22,514 GPU-hours on 24 cards in the first phase and 96 in the second. Methods include FP8 precision, the MuonH optimizer, and curriculum model averaging. Artifacts are released under Apache 2.0, with upstream datasets keeping their own terms. The authors project that a fitted scaling law reaches Qwen2-1.5B quality for about $4,400.

**Why China can scale this:** Chinese labs face export limits on data-center GPUs and buy consumer cards in volume. A published recipe that fits that hardware lets any lab with roughly 100 gaming GPUs copy it, and open release lets other labs reuse the pipeline.

**Global implications:** Pretraining a small model from scratch no longer requires a data-center budget, if the released code and data hold up. This is an Early signal: one preprint, 2 billion parameters, and benchmark claims based on the authors' own evaluation.

**Monday morning action:** Ask your ML lead to price a from-scratch small model against current fine-tuning spend and to reproduce one training phase.

Source: [arXiv 2608.27370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27370)

## Huawei Cloud Launches Cluster Service for 100,000-Card AI Training, Domestic Demand First

**What happened:** Tech Wire Asia reports that Huawei Cloud launched AI Cluster Service (AICS) in China on September 30, with international availability on November 30. Huawei claims support for training runs longer than 40 days, fault recovery within 10 minutes, and 20 percent higher token throughput than the prior generation, on clusters of more than 100,000 cards using its UnifiedBus interconnect. It also introduced Context Memory Storage for AI agents. The report cites over 3,500 customers and names healthcare, manufacturing, finance, embodied AI, and AI for science as target industries. The figures are Huawei's, and the report cites no independent benchmark.

**Why China can scale this:** Huawei sells chip, interconnect, cluster service, and industry software as one stack. Rotating chairman Eric Xu said the company lacks enough capacity to meet demand in China, which shows domestic enterprise demand already exceeds supply.

**Global implications:** Buyers in Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America may see a Huawei-hosted cluster offer from November 30, subject to local policy and export rules. The capacity shortage limits near-term volume.

**Monday morning action:** Map which accounts in Huawei Cloud markets could receive a Huawei-hosted cluster offer, and what your service terms would need to match.

Source: [Tech Wire Asia](https://techwireasia.com/2026/09/huawei-cloud-aics-enterprise-ai-agents/)
