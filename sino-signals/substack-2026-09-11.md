# Sino-Signals | Friday, September 11, 2026 — Two Ways to Route Around a Moat

*A Peking University chip beats an A100 by turning a device defect into arithmetic, and Alibaba sends its AI employees to work inside its rivals' apps.*

Both of today's signals make the same move in different registers. One team routed around a lithography constraint by changing what the hardware is asked to do. The other gave up the walled garden it spent a decade building. Neither is a frontier-model story, and that is the point.

## Peking University Builds the First Chip That Models a Brain at Brain Speed

A team led by Professor Yang Yuchao at Peking University, working with the Shanghai Institute of Microsystem and Information Technology under the Chinese Academy of Sciences, published a neural dynamical system chip in *Science*. The chip completes a single integration step in 2.12 milliseconds and reconstructs cortical white and gray matter surfaces up to 478 times faster than an NVIDIA A100 GPU, drawing 11.75 to 24.73 times less power than the best comparable ASICs. It was fabricated on a 40-nanometer process, and the in-memory computing and conductance-drift arrays occupy 0.28 square millimeters. The design exploits the predictable conductance drift of phase-change memory instead of correcting for it, turning a known device flaw into the computation itself.

China has funded memristor and in-memory computing research for a decade through Peking University, Tsinghua, and CAS institutes, and brain-computer interfaces entered the 15th Five-Year Plan as one of six designated future industries. A 40-nanometer node sits well inside what SMIC produces today, so this architecture scales on domestic fabs while advanced-node access stays restricted.

Western AI hardware strategy assumes performance comes from smaller transistors and bigger GPU clusters. Here device physics does the arithmetic, and a mature node beats a leading-edge GPU on a specific workload by two orders of magnitude. If Chinese labs keep finding workloads where analog in-memory computing wins, export controls on advanced lithography will protect less of the frontier than Washington and Brussels assume.

Monday morning: ask your hardware team which latency-critical workloads are dominated by data movement rather than raw arithmetic, because those go first. Medical imaging, surgical robotics, and BCI vendors should read the paper this quarter and decide whether to license, partner, or build.

## Alibaba Sends AI Digital Employees Into ByteDance and Tencent Workplaces

Alibaba Cloud released a new version of QoderWake on September 8, 2026. A user describes a role in one line and gets a working AI colleague. Those agents now run inside all three major Chinese workplace platforms, Alibaba's DingTalk, ByteDance's Feishu, and Tencent's WeCom, pulling context autonomously from shared documents, calendars, and group chats. Ten roles ship pre-tuned, including product manager, data analyst, UI designer, and front-end and back-end developer. Since the April release, Alibaba says it has deployed close to 100,000 digital employees that executed roughly 2 million tasks over three months.

DingTalk, Feishu, and WeCom together reach most of China's white-collar workforce, so an agent that crosses all three inherits a distribution base no Western equivalent can match. Alibaba gave up the walled garden at the application layer because the revenue now sits in workflow execution rather than seat licenses.

Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce each sell agents that work best inside their own suite, and each treats that lock-in as the moat. Alibaba has priced interoperability as the feature instead, which resets what a buyer will accept. Procurement teams in Europe and Southeast Asia will start asking whether an agent runs across Teams, Slack, and Google Workspace.

Monday morning: audit which AI agent contracts assume a single collaboration suite, and price a cross-platform requirement at renewal. The list of 10 pre-tuned roles is a public forecast of which knowledge-work tasks get automated first.

Source: [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3366919/alibaba-sends-ai-digital-employees-work-rival-apps-bytedance-tencent)

Put the two together and you get a working method. When the constraint is physical, change the physics you compute with. When the constraint is commercial, give away the thing your rivals are still defending and take the workflow instead. Both moves start from an honest reading of what you actually cannot have, which is a discipline that abundance tends to erode.
