Two signals today describe the same pattern from different layers of the stack. Both claims below rest on single reported sources, and each entry flags what remains unverified.

## China's Energy Storage Market Shrinks at Home While Overseas Orders Jump 83%

**What happened:** CNESA DataLink half-year data, reported by Energy-Storage.news, shows China commissioned 21.81 GW and 58.60 GWh of new-type energy storage in the first half of 2026. That is down 18 percent in power and 16 percent in energy from a year earlier, the first decline on record. Cumulative capacity reached 237.7 GW by the end of June, up 41.7 percent. Overseas contracts totaled 298 GWh, up 83 percent, with Europe leading and growth in the Middle East, India, and Chile. The source cites narrower spot price spreads, higher charging costs, and a national capacity pricing mechanism listed as Document No. 114. That document number has not been checked against the Chinese primary text.

**Why China can scale this:** Standalone projects made up 69.3 percent of additions, and a capacity tariff lets them combine capacity, energy, and ancillary revenue. A 237.7 GW fleet gives suppliers the largest set of operating data and reference sites, and surplus cell output can move quickly into export contracts.

**Global implications:** Chinese bids in European tenders are priced off a very large domestic base. The 298 GWh figure counts contracts, not installed capacity, and one report supports it.

**Monday morning action:** Ask each Chinese bidder what share of its contracted GWh has been delivered, and compare duration and warranty terms with your European tenders.

Source: [Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/chinas-half-year-energy-storage-deployments-post-first-ever-decline-to-22gw-59gwh-while-overseas-orders-surge-83/)

## DeepSeek Opens Its Software Toolkit to Huawei Chips, Targeting Nvidia's Moat

**What happened:** The South China Morning Post reported on September 30 that DeepSeek open-sourced six software modules for Huawei's Ascend AI processors. The one the article names is TileLang, a programming language for high-performance computing kernels. Its Ascend version adds native code generation, automatic scheduling, and synchronization for the Ascend 950 accelerator. DeepSeek describes the aim as an independent and controllable software ecosystem. The article does not name the other five modules or report benchmarks, so this entry makes no performance claim.

**Why China can scale this:** Huawei supplies hardware, DeepSeek supplies widely used open code, and universities and startups can adopt both without a license fee. A shared toolchain lets each lab reuse the same kernels and scheduling code.

**Global implications:** Part of Nvidia's lead rests on CUDA, the software developers already know. A maintained open alternative lowers switching costs for buyers outside the US export-control perimeter.

**Monday morning action:** Identify which parts of your AI stack depend on CUDA-only code, and assign one team to test an open kernel language on non-Nvidia hardware.

Source: [South China Morning Post](https://scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3369301/chinas-deepseek-open-sources-tools-help-huawei-chips-supplant-nvidia-ai)

China's storage makers are turning a slowing home market into export growth, while DeepSeek is working to make domestic chips usable at scale. Watch whether the 298 GWh converts into deliveries, and whether outside developers adopt the Ascend tools.
