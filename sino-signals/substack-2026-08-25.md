# Sino-Signals | Tuesday, August 25, 2026 — Ten Billion Dollars, One Use of Proceeds

*Alibaba raises US$10 billion and assigns all of it to AI, while iFlytek prepares a flagship model trained entirely on Chinese chips.*

Two announcements this week describe the same strategy from opposite ends. One is about how much capital China's platforms will commit to AI infrastructure. The other is about whether that infrastructure can be domestic all the way down to the accelerator. Together they set the terms Western competitors will price against in 2027.

## Alibaba Raises US$10 Billion and Commits Every Dollar to AI

Alibaba priced a placement of 710 million new Hong Kong shares at HK$112.70 each on August 23, 2026, raising HK$80 billion, about US$10.21 billion, with closing set for August 26. The company will use 100 percent of net proceeds to invest in its full stack AI capabilities and expand its AI infrastructure. The book covered close to three times, roughly 40 percent went to long only and sovereign wealth investors, and the Hong Kong shares fell 8 percent once the deal was public. This is Alibaba's first equity raise since its 2019 Hong Kong listing, and press reports describe it as the largest primary follow on ever by a Hong Kong listed company.

Alibaba can spend this money inside a stack it owns end to end, from the Qwen models through Alibaba Cloud down to its own silicon. On August 21 it told investors that its second generation T-Head accelerator will tape out and enter production this year. Beijing has pushed the major platforms toward domestic chips, so the capital converts into domestic capacity instead of an export license queue.

One company raising ten billion dollars of fresh equity and assigning all of it to AI infrastructure resets what counts as a serious capital commitment. It also moves where AI capital gets priced, since Hong Kong just absorbed a follow on that sits alongside Alphabet's and Intel's on this year's global league table. Expect Alibaba Cloud capacity, and the Qwen models running on it, to get cheaper and more available through 2027.

Monday morning: pull your 2027 AI infrastructure budget, find the line that assumes what inference capacity will cost, and test it against a competitor that just bought ten billion dollars of it.

## iFlytek Will Ship a Flagship Model Trained Only on Chinese Chips

iFlytek president Wu Xiaoru told the company's half year results briefing on August 21 that iFlytek will launch a new flagship general purpose model, trained entirely on domestic compute, at its 1024 Developer Festival in October, with a staged version at the end of August for developers to test. The company claims the model reaches the domestic first tier on coding ability and on cost per token. In April, Wu said the October model would run on Huawei's Ascend 950 platform and would be the first Chinese flagship benchmarked directly against the world's leading models. iFlytek reported first half revenue of 11.62 billion yuan, up 6.52 percent, with a net loss of 204 million yuan, narrowed by 14.68 percent.

iFlytek is the only mainstream Chinese model maker running full stack training and inference on domestic accelerators, a position held since it built the Feixing compute platform with Huawei. Its distribution runs through schools, hospitals, courts, and government procurement, where a fully domestic stack is a qualifying requirement and the customer is already committed to buying it.

Export controls rest on the premise that Chinese labs training on domestic silicon stay a generation behind. A flagship shipped on Ascend 950 and benchmarked against the leading Western models tests that premise, and October is when it gets graded. If the claim holds, the compute chokepoint becomes a cost difference, which is a much weaker instrument.

Monday morning: put the October festival on your watch list, plan to benchmark the released model yourself, and ask your policy and supply chain leads what changes if Chinese frontier training runs entirely on Chinese hardware by 2027.
