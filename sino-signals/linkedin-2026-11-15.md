When you book a ride through one app and another company sends the car, who checks that both the driver and the vehicle are licensed?

China’s Ministry of Transport counted 324 million orders completed through ride aggregators in September. Its November report ranks seven of those services by the share of rides where both permits were in place. Amap came first. The ministry gives the order of the platforms without publishing their individual percentages.

The decision to rank the app passengers use matters. A corporate travel manager can put that intermediary on the same side of the table as the operator supplying the car and ask each to account for a particular trip. The ranking itself tells a buyer little about whether the passenger arrived safely or on time.

Thinking futureback about this signal, would your travel team renew a booking contract if nobody could produce both permits for a sampled trip? If the app owns the customer relationship but the operator holds the records, who can put a passenger’s complaint right?
