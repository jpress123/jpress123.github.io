If a surgeon can operate from 8,000 kilometres away, what must the team beside the patient be able to do on its own?

On October 30, Tongji Hospital surgeon Yang Jun sat at a console in Antalya, Türkiye. In Wuhan, his colleagues were in the operating room with a patient undergoing prostate cancer surgery. Yang controlled the robot in real time. Tongji reports that the patient recovered and went home.

What deserves attention is how the hospital prepares people for that division of work. Tongji serves as a provincial training base and has held two specialist training sessions in robotic surgery. A successful case tells a Western hospital board what one experienced team achieved. It leaves open what staffing a smaller hospital would need to offer the same service.

Thinking futureback about this signal, would your board fund the bedside team’s training as readily as the remote console? If the connection drops, who has the authority and the skills to take over, and which hospitals could afford to offer patients that assurance?
