Sino-Signals | November 10, 2026 — What do you see before the answer appears?

A teacher in Jinan prepares to explain a cylinder’s volume. A salon customer in Shanghai looks at a magnified image of their scalp. In both settings a screen can bring something difficult to see into focus. The harder design decision is when to offer an interpretation and when to let the person looking make one.

## Teachers build the explanation

On November 5, teachers at Bole Experimental School in Jinan used a workshop to make interactive courseware. Their geometry example showed how to derive the volume of a cylinder. Staff also practised setting up workspaces for teaching and grade-level administration. The school’s account describes what participants did during training and leaves open how pupils subsequently used the materials.

The choice of a shared problem matters. When colleagues compare two versions of the same explanation, they can discuss a particular transition. Does the learner see why a rearranged shape retains its volume? At which point does the animation ask them to take that on trust? Those are questions a subject teacher can answer by changing a frame and trying the lesson again.

A school elsewhere could evaluate competing tools using the same exercise. Give each teacher time to make one explanation, then pause it before the formula appears. Ask pupils to draw or describe what comes next. Look for the first step on which their accounts differ. The resulting discussion gives the teacher a reason to keep, replace or slow down a particular part of the lesson. It also gives the purchasing team a better question to ask than how long it took to produce a slide. [Read the school’s report](https://shandong.eol.cn/sdjc/202611/t20261106_2791330.shtml)

## A salon customer sees the scan

At Shanghai’s import expo, an International Finance News reporter tried the Kérastase scalp scanner. In under five minutes, magnified images appeared alongside a report scoring the condition of the scalp. The brand presented the scan as the opening step in its K-SPA service. The accompanying K-STATION care device was developed with a Chinese startup after a connection at an earlier edition of the expo.

A stylist can use the display to show a customer what prompted a recommendation. There is also a commercial question in the order of presentation. Once a score appears, it can be hard for the customer to distinguish a feature they can see from a judgement they are being asked to accept. The reporter observed the consultation and its output. That experience cannot establish what difference buying a treatment would make.

For a retailer reviewing a similar service, watch one appointment all the way through to the decision at the till. Give the customer a chance to describe what they saw before the stylist explains the score. Afterwards, ask what the image showed, which conclusion came from the software and which choice the customer felt able to make. [Read the reporter’s account](https://www.ifnews.com/news.html?aid=884860&cid=49)

In a classroom the pupil has to reconstruct an argument. In a salon the customer has to decide whether to spend money. In each case the person designing the screen decides how much room there is for a second interpretation.
