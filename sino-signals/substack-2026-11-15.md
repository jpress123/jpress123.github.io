# Sino-Signals | November 15, 2026 — Who can check the claim?

A transport regulator counts rides with two valid permits. A chemistry teacher asks pupils to challenge a battery proposal against the research behind it.

Consider two questions that sound easier than they are. Was the car that actually collected the passenger licensed for that trip? Does the paper an AI tool drew on support the material it has proposed? In each case, the answer depends on whether someone can follow the claim back to a specific record.

## China Ranks Ride Aggregators by Driver and Vehicle Permits

China’s transport ministry reported on November 11 that ride aggregators completed 324 million orders in September. It ranked seven aggregators by the share of rides for which both driver and vehicle held permits. Amap ranked first. The ministry gave no individual platform percentages.

The national reporting system received 981 million ride orders for September. Reporting on aggregators separately makes the passenger-facing intermediary visible alongside the operator supplying the ride. The distinction matters because the company whose name appears on a passenger’s phone can differ from the company that dispatches the car. A passenger trying to resolve a problem is likely to start with the service through which they made the booking.

A travel buyer comparing booking services can ask how often both credentials were valid for the ride actually supplied. A licensing ranking alone says nothing about crashes, waiting times or service quality. For a corporate travel programme, the next useful question is which party can reconstruct the trip. A provider might be able to show that its partner generally uses licensed cars while finding it much harder to identify the vehicle that replaced a cancelled booking on a particular evening.

Pick a sample of bookings made through an intermediary. Ask who checked both permits at dispatch and who can retrieve the record when a passenger raises a concern. Include a booking where the first driver cancelled. That makes it possible to see whether the system checked the replacement or simply carried forward a record from the original booking.

## Haidian Chemistry Students Challenge AI Battery Proposals

At a November 8 teaching forum, Peng Dan described a chemistry lesson in which students assessed AI proposals for solid electrolytes in a mock research tender. A collection of more than 20 research papers informed the AI exercise. Haidian Education published the account on November 13. In the exercise the machine could put forward a hypothesis. The students’ job was to find weaknesses in it, a different task from asking a model to summarise the reading list.

Teachers at the Beijing school demonstrated different uses of AI across subjects. A chemistry department elsewhere could reuse the tender format while choosing papers appropriate to its students. A teacher can choose which part of a paper is within reach of a class and where learners need help interpreting a measurement.

The exercise gives science teachers a way to assess whether students can challenge a plausible materials claim. The report describes classroom practice and provides no comparative learning results. A department assessing a similar lesson could look at the students’ reasons for rejecting a claim. Some may notice a missing measurement. Others may realise that a result obtained under one set of conditions is being used to predict performance under another.

Give students two competing proposals and access to the papers behind them. Ask which claim the evidence supports and which would still require an experiment. Keep the initial proposals alongside the students’ revisions. A teacher can then assess who recognised the uncertainty and how each student chose the next piece of evidence to request.

[Haidian Education](https://www.bjhdedu.cn/xw/jwdt/202611/t20261113_97093.html)

A transport buyer does not need to inspect every car to ask a better question of a platform. A student does not need to build a battery to identify a claim the available evidence cannot support. Both need a record they can inspect and the authority to ask for a better answer.
