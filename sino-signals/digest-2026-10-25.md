## The pick of the week

### China Proposes AI Export Curbs and Starts Technology Registration

**🏛️ Policy & Governance | Emerging | Impact 4 of 5**

On October 16, the Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology issued Announcement 2026 No. 48, consulting on 40 technology additions to the export catalogue, including 23 AI technologies. Comments close November 15; temporary pre-export registration applies from publication to covered transfers, including transactions already underway but incomplete. The proposed catalogue changes are not yet final prohibitions.

**Why it scales in China.** The notice uses a central registration system rather than requiring a separate industry rollout. Our interpretation is that this gives authorities a common view of covered technology transfers across companies, while the proposed exclusion for technology already in the public domain preserves an important distinction for open research.

**What it means outside China.** Our assessment is that technology ownership and deployment structure become more important procurement questions for multinational firms. A foreign bank evaluating a Chinese AI supplier should distinguish access to a service from a transfer of controlled technology, and have counsel assess the actual transaction rather than assume every model is banned.

**Monday morning.** Inventory planned Chinese AI technology transfers and licensing arrangements, then ask counsel and suppliers to check the notice and attachments against each transaction. Record dependencies and an alternative deployment plan before signing or changing ownership.

Source: [Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology, Announcement 2026 No. 48](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/dwmygl/art/2026/art_61446ab4beee4f63973d4d0ab3097648.html)
