What happens to a storage industry when its home market shrinks for the first time and its export orders jump 83 percent?

CNESA DataLink half-year data, reported by Energy-Storage.news, shows China commissioned 21.81 GW and 58.60 GWh of new-type energy storage in the first half of 2026. That is down 18 percent in power and 16 percent in energy from a year earlier, the first decline on record. Cumulative capacity stands at 237.7 GW. Overseas contracts reached 298 GWh, up 83 percent, with Europe as the core market. The source cites narrower spot-market price spreads, higher charging costs, and a national capacity pricing mechanism for standalone storage, listed as Document No. 114. I have not checked that document number against the Chinese primary text.

Why it matters in China: standalone projects, 69.3 percent of additions, now earn from capacity tariffs, energy markets, and ancillary services. Demand follows project returns instead of installation targets. Suppliers with a 237.7 GW reference fleet can redirect surplus output to export bids.

Global impact for Western leaders: Chinese bids for European tenders are priced off a very large domestic base. The 83 percent figure measures contracts, and installed capacity will follow later. One report supports these numbers, so treat them as a strong indicator and not as a final count.

Also today: DeepSeek open-sourced six software modules for Huawei Ascend chips, led by a TileLang version for the Ascend 950, according to the South China Morning Post. The aim is a software stack with no Nvidia dependency.

Monday morning action: if you buy or finance storage, ask each Chinese bidder what share of its contracted GWh has been delivered, and compare duration and warranty terms with your European tenders.
