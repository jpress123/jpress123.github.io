When a 175-year-old information company rips out its frontier AI vendor and rebuilds on Chinese open weights, is that a cost decision or a capability decision?

Thomson Reuters has built an in-house model, Thomson-1, on top of Snowdon, a system it developed by adapting Alibaba's open-source Qwen. It now handles document-review work that previously ran on Anthropic's Claude. Harvey, the legal tech firm backed by OpenAI, Sequoia, and Andreessen Horowitz, post-trained its new model Harvey Tenet on Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3, and reports it beats both its base model and US frontier systems, including Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, on complex legal agentic tasks. Ramp's AI Index shows the share of AI-spending businesses paying for model serving platforms rose to 6.1 percent in July, up from 4.5 percent in January.

Why this works in China: the labs ship frontier-class open weights on a monthly cadence and price aggressively underneath. Z.AI this week revealed it built the anonymous benchmark performer Ox Alpha, renamed it GLM-5.3-Flash, and set a public price of 15 cents per million input tokens and 50 cents per million output. Moonshot is negotiating with Microsoft, Amazon, and Google to host Kimi K3 on their clouds, asking for up to 30 percent of K3-related service revenue.

What it means for Western leaders: the premium tier has found its ceiling. Fable 5, the most advanced model on the market, accounts for only 6 percent of the tokens businesses buy from Anthropic and 11.4 percent of dollars spent there. Two named professional-services incumbents have now concluded that a specialized Chinese open base outperforms a general American frontier model on their own work.

Monday morning: split your AI workloads into the ones that need frontier reasoning and the ones that need a good-enough model whose weights you control. Price a fine-tuned open-weight substitute for your highest-volume task, and put a date on the bake-off before your procurement cycle closes.
