## The pick of the week

### China Opens a Cold War Style AI Hotline With Washington

**🏛️ Policy & Governance | Accelerating | Impact 5 of 5**

Xi Jinping's three-day state visit to Washington concluded September 26 with the White House announcing a US-China Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue, a bilateral communication channel for AI incidents modeled on Cold War-era emergency hotlines. Chinese Vice-Premier He Lifeng and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent completed the first round of the underlying AI dialogue on September 20 during the eighth round of US-China trade talks in New York, which Commerce Ministry spokesman He Yadong confirmed on September 24. The next SI Dialogue session is scheduled by November 2026, announced alongside a $30 billion tariff cut agreed during the summit.

**Why it scales in China.** Beijing used the dialogue to negotiate from parity rather than catch-up: Xi told Trump the two nations share "the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good," and Foreign Minister Wang Yi framed the summit's goal as putting US-China relations on "equal footing." The channel gives Beijing an institutional forum to contest Washington's chip export restrictions and its accusations that Chinese labs used "distillation" to replicate American models, moving that fight from unilateral export-control actions to head-of-state diplomacy.

**What it means outside China.** This is the first formal government-to-government AI incident channel between the two AI superpowers, but its scope is undefined, neither side has specified what qualifies as an "SI incident" or what notification either side owes the other. For Western governments, it signals Washington now treats Beijing as a dialogue partner on frontier AI safety rather than solely a target of containment, a framing that will shape how the EU, UK, and other AI Safety Institute members structure their own bilateral tracks with China.

**Monday morning.** Companies running dual US-China AI strategies should read this as a signal that export-control enforcement could ease incrementally as trust-building continues, not as detente, the incident-notification scope remains undefined and could tighten again quickly. Government affairs teams should watch the November 2026 session for the first concrete test of what the channel actually does.
