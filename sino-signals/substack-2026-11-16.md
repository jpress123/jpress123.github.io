# Sino-Signals | November 16, 2026 — Who has the evidence when AI fails?

A Beijing creator challenged a platform’s decision to hide an answer. A Shenzhen telecoms provider bought insurance against interruptions to AI services. Both situations turn on what someone can prove after a system has failed them.

## Beijing Court Orders Platform to Restore a Creator’s Hidden Answer

In a case presented on November 10, a platform hid a user’s answer and imposed a one-day posting ban after flagging unlabelled AI use. The court found that the platform had failed to substantiate its decision and ordered it to restore the answer and erase the violation record. The Beijing News reports that the platform relied on a machine check and human review. The user had written the answer spontaneously, and the court considered it unreasonable to require earlier drafts as evidence of authorship.

The court included the dispute among seven examples of platform governance. Its reasoning gives moderation teams a concrete case for deciding what evidence to retain when a creator appeals. For an editor, the difficult moment comes when a human reviewer agrees with the software but cannot say which feature of the work led to that conclusion.

Creative platforms that use detectors have to decide what a reviewer can explain to a writer. An appeal is difficult to resolve if only the platform can inspect the evidence and the writer composed the text directly online. A writer who composes in the platform’s own reply box may have exactly one record of the work. Asking that writer to produce a separate file shifts the burden to the person least able to meet it.

Take one disputed moderation decision through the appeal process. Ask the reviewer to explain the evidence in ordinary language and demonstrate how a reversal removes the penalty from the account. Restoring the text and clearing the account history are separate actions. A team can check whether both occurred before it closes an appeal.

## PICC Pairs Server Cover With AI Service Interruption Insurance

PICC said its Shenzhen branch signed an insurance package with CITIC-group telecoms provider 中企通信. Its November 15 account describes two policies covering computing-centre property damage and specified losses when AI services fail, stop or underperform. One part concerns the equipment that runs the service. The other concerns financial exposure when a customer cannot use the service as expected.

A provider selling model access can face both a hardware repair bill and customer claims over interrupted service. The signed package brings those exposures into one insurance purchase. It matters that an insurer and a named provider have signed a package. A buyer can now ask an underwriter how the two parts would interact in a particular incident. The public description alone cannot answer whether any particular customer would receive compensation.

Buyers of model access can compare their service agreements with the events their suppliers have insured. The announcement does not disclose premiums, limits or a paid claim. Consider a provider whose equipment continues running while responses become too slow for a customer to use. Its operations team may regard the service as available while a customer has already stopped processing orders. An underwriter, supplier and customer may each need different evidence about the same interval.

Trace a hypothetical outage from the first failed request through to customer compensation. Mark where the service agreement promises a remedy and where the insurance wording actually responds. Have the operations team identify the records it keeps today. If a buyer would need timestamps or service measurements the supplier discards, that is a contract negotiation worth having before the next outage.

The writer needs an explanation that can change a moderation decision. The service customer needs a record that can support a claim. In both cases the organisation with the most useful evidence can decide in advance who will keep it, who can inspect it and who has authority to act on it.
