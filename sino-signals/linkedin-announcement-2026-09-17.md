I am back in Shanghai for the new academic year.

I spent the summer teaching and talking across Europe and the United States. Four things stayed with me.

China came up in almost every room, and almost always as a race. Rarely as a source to learn from.

Sustainability had quietly slid down the agenda, right as AI budgets climbed.

Institutions were still running pilots. Their Chinese counterparts had already deployed.

And the mood was anxiety. Job loss, falling behind, what we stand to lose. Very little energy for what we could build.

That last one is what sent me back to a habit I started in January. Read what China's own sources say about AI, in Chinese, every morning. Write down what a Western leader would need to know by Monday.

Sino-Signals is that record. 207 signals across 90 days, each verified against a primary source, organized by systemic category, industry lens, and strategic urgency.

I keep it for one reason. We share a planet, and the problems that matter most cross every border we have drawn. We need all the encouragement we can get to use AI for good, to adopt more sustainable solutions, and to rebuild common understanding across cultures.

Reading China closely is one way to do that. Not to keep score. To widen the sense of what is possible, and to see which parts of the future are already running somewhere else.

Some of what I find is uncomfortable. Some of it should be borrowed. All of it is more interesting than the race.

It is a working archive, free and open. I get things wrong and correct them in public.
