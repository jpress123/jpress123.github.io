Who gets to stop an AI system when a banking decision goes wrong?

Bank of Communications is making that a leadership question. In an October 28 account, CIO Liu Lei described AI applications in cross-border finance and wealth management, while calling for staff participation, redesigned processes and risk backstops.

His prescription includes monitoring, circuit breakers and fallback arrangements. This is the bank's stated approach, not independent proof of better outcomes.

In China, the interesting signal is how a major bank connects AI expansion with organisational responsibility. The people delivering the service need a role in shaping how it works and when it stops.

For leaders elsewhere, that makes service design a boardroom issue. Faster processing matters, but so does knowing who handles an exception, explains a decision and restores a dependable service.

Thinking futureback about this signal, consider running a small trial in one banking workflow. Bring frontline staff and risk colleagues together. Define who can pause the system, rehearse the fallback, and track customer outcomes alongside time saved.
