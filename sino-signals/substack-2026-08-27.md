## China Grades Data Centers on Efficiency as AI Power Use Jumps 30%

At the 2026 Green Computing (AI) Conference in Hohhot on August 22, the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology and the National Data Administration reported that China's computing facilities consumed 170 billion kWh of electricity in 2025, up roughly 30 percent year on year. More than 160 data centers now hold 4A or higher green ratings, average PUE at hyperscale sites has fallen to 1.34, and 42 clusters of 10,000 accelerators or more are operating. Ao Li, vice president of CAICT, framed the problem as one for the whole chain rather than the individual facility. During the conference, Hohhot and Ulanqab signed 13 projects worth 136.1 billion yuan spanning chip testing, equipment manufacturing, compute centers, and token factories.

China can scale this because compute-electricity coordination, suan dian xie tong, entered the government work report for the first time this year, turning a technical preference into a provincial mandate. Hohhot reports 7.12 million kW of installed new energy capacity, green electricity use above 85 percent at its data centers, and power at roughly 0.35 yuan per kWh. The National Energy Administration expects national compute electricity demand to rise by more than 100 billion kWh a year through the 15th Five-Year Plan, so siting, tariffs, and grid buildout are decided in one cycle for a decade.

For Western operators the competitive metric shifts from cluster size to delivered cost per token under a carbon constraint. Daily token calls in China reached about 140 trillion by March 2026, against roughly 100 billion in early 2024, a thousandfold rise in two years. Hyperscalers negotiating multi-year interconnection queues are competing with a system where green certificate trading, direct clean power supply, and carbon accounting are coordinated by the same planners who approve the site.

Monday morning: ask your infrastructure team for cost per million tokens including electricity and carbon, then find out where the power for your next training run comes from, when it connects, and who is ahead of you in the queue.

## Noitom Releases 617 Hours of Precision Motion Data Free to Robotics Researchers

Noitom Robotics released HiPHI on August 20 at the World Robot Conference in Beijing: 617.5 hours of optical motion capture from 132 performers, recorded at 90 Hz with sub-millimeter marker tracking. The release splits into 371.8 hours of whole-body human motion and 245.7 hours of human-object interaction, where each object's trajectory and mesh are logged in sync with the performer, indexed by motion units drawn from FrameNet. It ships free on Hugging Face under the ModalityNet Open Research License, with an academic paper at arXiv 2608.16222 and commercial terms through modalitynet.com. Policies trained on HiPHI already run on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, which runs, sits, crawls, carries a box, and pulls a suitcase. Chief of R&D Lei Han said coverage, per-frame quality, and object state decide whether a humanoid can learn from motion data at all, so those are the numbers the company published.

China can scale this because the release is a sample of a running commercial pipeline. Noitom says its capture infrastructure produces more than 100,000 hours a year, and further releases are planned, starting with an omni-modal interaction corpus later in 2026. This year's World Robot Conference drew 373 exhibitors and more than 150 product debuts, putting the data producers and the robot buyers in the same hall.

High-precision human motion data has been the scarcest input in embodied AI, and it is normally guarded as a proprietary asset. Publishing it sets a public benchmark that Western labs get measured against, and moves the competitive question from who owns the data to who owns the capture infrastructure that keeps producing it. The same corpus feeds digital humans and computer graphics, so film, games, and animation pipelines inherit the standard too.

Monday morning: download HiPHI and benchmark your own motion library against its published coverage and quality numbers before funding another capture campaign, and price again any roadmap that treats proprietary motion data as a moat.
