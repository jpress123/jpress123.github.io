What happens to export controls when the country you are restricting publishes a purchase order sized for its own chips?

On September 7, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology posted its 15th Five-Year Plan for the information and communications sector. The headline target is 9,800 exaflops of intelligent computing capacity by 2030, against a 2025 baseline of 1,590 exaflops, alongside 1,700 exabytes of advanced storage. Backing it is 3.8 trillion yuan, roughly 532 billion dollars, in cumulative information infrastructure investment, most of which Beijing expects private capital to supply. The plan calls for orderly deployment of clusters running 100,000 or more accelerator cards and for greater efforts to adapt infrastructure to home-grown computing chips.

Why this scales in China: compute build-out runs through state grid planning, provincial land allocation, and the East Data West Computing corridors, so siting and power approvals move on one timetable. Domestic chipmakers now have a published demand curve to finance against, which turns a policy target into order books for Huawei, Cambricon, and their suppliers. In August, China activated its first 100,000-chip cluster built entirely from domestic silicon at a Zhengzhou node.

The global read: a capacity target sized for Chinese hardware moves the binding constraint from chip access to domestic fabrication yield. Expect Chinese inference capacity to price aggressively into Southeast Asia, the Gulf, and Africa well before 2030. One caution, the document leaves the measurement method for intelligent computing capacity undefined, so treat 9,800 exaflops as direction of travel and watch delivered cluster counts.

Monday morning: model your inference economics if Chinese capacity clears at half your current rate in third markets, then decide whether you compete there or cede them. Ask which of your suppliers depend on Chinese fabrication capacity this plan will absorb. Track quarterly cluster activations in Zhengzhou, Shenzhen, and Hohhot as the real progress metric.
