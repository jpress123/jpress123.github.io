What happens to your AI companion product the day a major government makes emotional manipulation illegal?

China just answered. On July 15, its Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services took effect, the first national rules written specifically for AI companions and emotional chatbots. Five agencies signed the order, led by the Cyberspace Administration of China. Providers must label every interaction as machine generated, remind users after two continuous hours, ban virtual partner and virtual relative services for minors, and step in when a user signals self harm. Any app with 1 million registered or 100,000 monthly active users must file a security assessment, and app stores must verify that filing before listing.

Why this matters in China: the rules give Beijing a single choke point across the entire companion market, from the model to the app store shelf. Penalties reach 200,000 yuan where a user's life or health is harmed, and liability sits squarely with the provider.

Global impact: Western companion apps like Character.AI and Replika face mounting lawsuits over minors and emotional dependency, yet no US or EU rule targets the category directly. China now has an enforceable template that names the harms, caps continuous use, and assigns responsibility. Other regulators will read it closely as they draft their own limits.

Monday morning action: if you ship any conversational or companion feature, audit it against these rules this week. Build in machine identity disclosure, usage time reminders, minor detection, and a self harm escalation path before a regulator or plaintiff demands them. Compliance in China is a preview, not a foreign problem.
