If an online review says a jacket kept someone dry, what does an “AI-generated” label actually tell the buyer?

Ahead of the November 11 shopping festival, Guangzhou’s Nansha district issued guidance covering AI marketing images, customer-service scripts and virtual livestream hosts. It asks for prominent labels on generated marketing material. It also tells sellers they must not invent experiences when producing customer reviews.

Those instructions address two different moments in a purchase. A shopper might accept an illustration of a jacket and still reasonably expect the person describing a wet commute to have worn it. A platform can check for a label before publishing an ad. Checking the experience behind a testimonial requires someone to ask the seller for evidence.

Thinking futureback about this signal, who in a Western marketplace has the authority to take a bestselling review offline when nobody can identify the customer behind it? If platforms measure disclosure and stop there, who will be left to test the promise in the rain?
