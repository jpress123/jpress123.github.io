## China's Building Materials Giant Runs Industrial AI at 95 Percent Autonomy

The Digital Intelligence Research Institute of China National Building Material Group detailed a 1+1+N industrial large model now operating across more than 100 process manufacturing plants. Production agents hold real-time closed-loop control of core processes, the model's autonomous operation rate exceeds 95 percent, and each plant reports several million yuan in annual benefit. The architecture stacks one real-time data base, one industrial model that fuses data, mechanism, business logic, and safety layers, and clusters of agents across research, production, supply, sales, and service. The institute won a WAIC award for the deployment.

Cement, glass, chemicals, and steel run continuous and tightly coupled, the hardest setting for autonomous control, and a central state-owned group can mandate one proven stack across its own plants. Models are trained to grow out of the factory, then run in the cloud with edge inference wired directly to industrial control. The same agents are already extending into chemicals, steel, and agriculture.

Industrial AI from Siemens and Schneider still centers on engineering assistance and predictive maintenance, short of whole-plant autonomy with audited returns. Closed-loop control at 95 percent autonomy resets the benchmark, and gives Chinese heavy industry a compounding cost and yield advantage over rivals buying point solutions.

Monday morning, pick one continuous line, measure how much runs under autonomous closed-loop control today, and set a target against 95 percent. Ask every vendor for quantified per-plant ROI and safety fault tolerance.

## China Clears Its First Fully AI Made Feature Film for Licensed Streaming

On July 23, the AIGC feature Qitan: Paper Blade Crossing the Wasteland launched exclusively on iQiyi as the first fully AI-produced long-form film to clear China's Online Drama Distribution License. It is the first title to complete the entire pipeline, from AI full-process production to filing, content review, license, and platform release. A crew of about 20 people made it, including prompt engineers and AI asset artists alongside directors and screenwriters. Their workflow ran from prompt design and visual asset generation to AI redrawing and 4K output.

China has assembled a regulatory pipeline for AI content, from the 2023 generative AI measures to June 2026 rules that label and grade AI micro-dramas. That clarity lets studios, platforms, and investors treat AI feature production as a repeatable process, and iQiyi's distribution reach turns one approval into a template.

Hollywood and European studios meet AI filmmaking mostly through lawsuits and guild disputes, with no clear route to approve and release a fully AI-made feature. China has now shown the full loop of production, approval, and distribution. As AI compresses feature and animation budgets, the advantage moves to whoever industrializes the pipeline first.

Monday morning, map your AI production pipeline against a compliance and release checklist before greenlighting AI-heavy projects, and treat regulatory approval, not generation quality, as the gate to market.
