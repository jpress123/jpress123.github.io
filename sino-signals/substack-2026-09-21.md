Sino-Signals | Monday, September 21, 2026 — Beijing Writes the Coder Layoff Into Policy

China made job redesign a condition of its AI software subsidy, and Huawei showed what 550 kilowatts of saved power buys.

Two signals today sit at opposite ends of the same build-out. One decides who writes the software and what happens to the people who used to. The other decides what it runs on and what the electricity costs.

## China Pushes AI Coding Into 20,000 Software Firms While Protecting Coder Jobs

The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued the AI Plus Software Special Action Implementation Plan on September 11, document MIIT Xinfa 2026 No. 209. By 2028 it puts intelligent programming tools into 20,000 above-scale software enterprises, funds 100 intelligent retooling projects, and builds 100 benchmark agent-software applications. Wang Weiwei, deputy director of the ministry's Information Technology Development Department, framed the moment as a chance to change lanes and overtake. One clause separates the document from every other national AI plan: it commits to employment-friendly development and orders firms to redesign roles and retrain staff alongside deployment, so the software workforce avoids a sudden mass layoff.

Why China can scale this. The software and IT services industry earned 15.48 trillion yuan in 2025, and daily token calls passed 500 trillion by June 2026. Compute vouchers reimburse part of the cost when firms buy domestic coding tools, so adoption arrives with a subsidy attached. An agent-software app store and a Skills package library give 20,000 firms one distribution channel instead of 20,000 separate integrations.

Global implications. Western governments have left AI and software employment to the labor market and to company discretion. Beijing made job redesign a condition of the subsidy rather than a consequence of it. The plan also requires security review of AI-generated code and pre-launch testing in priority industries, an auditable standard that multinationals shipping into China will be measured against. Lyu Weifeng of the China Software Industry Association stated the trade plainly: entry-level coding roles compress while architecture, integration, and requirements work grows.

Monday morning. Ask your engineering leadership what share of your code is now machine-written and who reviews it for security before release. Decide whether your AI coding rollout carries a retraining commitment before your developers infer the answer themselves.

## Huawei Links 4,096 AI Chips in One Pod and Cuts 550 Kilowatts

David Wang, deputy chairman and rotating chairman of Huawei, launched the Atlas 960E SuperPoD at Huawei Connect in Shanghai on September 17. A single pod scales to 4,096 NPUs across unified memory addressing, delivering 8 EFLOPS at FP8 and 16 EFLOPS at FP4 for models approaching ten trillion parameters. Huawei replaced the 48,000 800G optical modules such a system would normally need with 5,500 Hi-ONE near-packaged optical engines at 7.2 terabits per second each, removing 550 kilowatts of draw and lifting availability to 99.8 percent. Linked over UnifiedBus, the pods form clusters of up to 512,000 NPUs, or a million with multi-rail topology.

Why China can scale this. Huawei owns the optical stack, the interconnect protocol, the silicon, and the liquid cooling, so it can trade one layer against another where a firm buying merchant parts has to negotiate. The compute build-out Beijing funded in September gives every large Chinese lab a reason to train on Ascend rather than wait for export licenses. CANN, the Ascend software layer, has moved to community-driven open source, which is how Huawei attacks the one moat where Nvidia still leads.

Global implications. Power and optics, not chip count, bound most Western data center expansions, and 550 kilowatts per pod changes site selection and utility negotiation. Near-packaged optics at mass-production volume arrived first in a Chinese system, resetting the reference design hyperscalers compare against. Every buyer weighing Nvidia against alternatives gains a credible second quote.

Monday morning. Have your infrastructure team recalculate cost per delivered FLOP with interconnect power included. If you procure AI capacity in Asia, the Middle East, or Africa, ask what an Ascend-based quote would look like.
