Your engineering team is rolling out AI coding tools this quarter. Has anyone written down what happens to the junior developers?

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology answered that question in policy on September 11. The AI Plus Software Special Action Implementation Plan, document MIIT Xinfa 2026 No. 209, puts intelligent programming tools into 20,000 above-scale software enterprises by 2028, funds 100 intelligent retooling projects, and builds 100 benchmark agent-software applications. Compute vouchers reimburse part of the cost when firms buy domestic coding tools.

One clause separates this from every other national AI plan. The document commits to employment-friendly development and requires firms to redesign roles and retrain staff alongside deployment, so the software workforce avoids a sudden mass layoff. Lyu Weifeng of the China Software Industry Association stated the trade without softening it: entry-level coding roles compress while architecture, integration, and requirements work grows.

Why it scales in China. The software and IT services industry earned 15.48 trillion yuan in 2025, and daily token calls passed 500 trillion by June 2026. An agent-software app store and a Skills package library give 20,000 firms one distribution channel instead of 20,000 separate integrations. The subsidy and the obligation arrive together.

Why it matters for Western leaders. We have left AI and software employment to the labor market and to company discretion. Beijing made job redesign a condition of the subsidy rather than a consequence of it. The plan also requires security review of AI-generated code and pre-launch testing for software in priority industries, which becomes a procurement standard that multinationals shipping into China will be measured against.

Monday morning. Ask your engineering leadership what share of your code is now machine-written and who reviews it for security before release. Decide whether your AI coding rollout carries a retraining commitment, and say so before your developers infer the answer themselves.
