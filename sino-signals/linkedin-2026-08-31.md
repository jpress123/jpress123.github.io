What does your newest factory cost to run if you designed it around 2,000 AI agents instead of around people? TCL just published the answer, and it ships an air conditioner every seven seconds.

TCL's Nansha industrial park in Guangzhou cost nearly 2 billion yuan, about 294 million US dollars. It holds three lights-out factories, 15 advanced laboratories, and 10 intelligent production lines. An on-site supercomputing center with 106 server cabinets ingests real-time data from 32,000 IoT sensors and runs more than 2,000 AI agents in parallel. Designed capacity is 8 million units a year, with roughly 4 million expected in 2026.

Why this scales in China: the country now counts more than 43,000 smart factories, and the eight-ministry AI Plus Manufacturing initiative from January 2026 targets 1,000 industrial AI agents and 100 industrial datasets by 2027. TCL put AI in the foundation instead of retrofitting models onto running lines, so agents supervise inspection, energy allocation, and scheduling from the first shift. Rival Gree cut one 480-meter assembly line from over 70 workers to 20.

Global impact: TCL reported European air conditioner sales up 30 percent year on year in the first half of 2026, with Britain, France, and the Nordics up 300 percent. Management plans to replicate the Nansha model at new bases in Thailand and Pakistan. Western appliance makers are competing against a cost structure set by software agents and a 50,000 square meter solar array, well past anything labor arbitrage explains.

Monday morning: ask your operations lead what an AI-native version of your newest plant would look like if it were designed from the sensor layer up. Price that against your current retrofit roadmap before a competitor exports its version into your market.
