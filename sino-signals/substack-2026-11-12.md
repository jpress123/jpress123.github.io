# Sino-Signals | November 12, 2026 — Who acts on the readings?

A hospital boiler and a delivery route pose the same management question: who can change the plan when better information arrives?

A hospital in Zhejiang has reported energy savings after replacing equipment that its facilities team could monitor through a carbon account. During Singles’ Day, a logistics operator reported how often dispatchers used routes recommended by its model. The figures measure different things, and each leaves a different question for the person signing off the next investment.

## Quzhou Hospital Uses Carbon Accounts to Target Energy Upgrades

Kecheng District People’s Hospital monitors electricity, gas and water through connected sensors. After changes including a heat pump and new chillers, China Daily reported an 8.22% overall energy-saving rate and over 506 tonnes of carbon emissions avoided in the first nine months of 2026. The hospital’s other changes included motion-sensitive lights in its underground parking garage. Someone arriving by car experiences one small part of a much larger change in how the hospital runs its buildings.

Quzhou reports 1,098 organisations with carbon accounts, including hospitals and schools. A shared measurement system gives their facilities teams a starting point, though each building needs its own assessment. A school and a hospital can use the same way of recording electricity use while making very different decisions about which loads can safely be reduced. In a hospital, dependable hot water matters around the clock.

Hospital leaders can connect metering to decisions about equipment replacement. The reported figures cover multiple measures and do not isolate the contribution of any single device. A finance committee evaluating another hospital should ask what period the comparison covers and how occupancy changed. For the board, the practical test is whether someone can move a proposal from the facilities desk into the capital budget.

Ask facilities and finance to trace one unusually high reading to a funded change. Agree how to verify savings while maintaining reliable heating and cooling. Ask the person responsible for the plant to explain what happens if the new equipment needs maintenance during the busiest week of the year.

## JD Uses Parcel Forecasts to Plan Singles’ Day Deliveries

People’s Daily reports that JD Logistics uses its Super Brain 3.0 model to forecast parcel volumes on express routes. During Singles’ Day reporting, forecast accuracy exceeded 95% and adoption of AI-recommended delivery routes exceeded 90%. The first figure concerns expected parcel volumes. The second describes whether people responsible for delivery used the suggested route. Neither tells a shopper whether a parcel arrived in the promised window.

A retail promotion concentrates demand across many delivery routes. Network planners can compare forecasts with actual parcel counts and see which recommendations dispatchers use. A dispatcher who rejects a recommendation can provide particularly valuable information. A road closure, a building’s receiving hours or a queue at a loading bay can change the best choice even when the volume forecast is sound.

Route acceptance measures use. Retailers assessing similar systems still need delivery times, missed promises and local exceptions to judge customer outcomes. Procurement teams should ask how an operator counts a route that was accepted and then changed halfway through the shift. A retailer comparing providers needs a consistent definition before putting the figure into a service agreement.

Sample one peak-period route and compare the recommendation with the dispatcher’s choice. Record whether the alternative protected a delivery window. Then look separately at missed deliveries on routes that dispatchers accepted as suggested. That comparison gives the team a reason to examine both kinds of decision without rewarding agreement for its own sake.

[People’s Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1112/c1004-40832336.html)

In Quzhou the important handoff runs from the person who sees the meter to the person who funds a boiler replacement. At JD it runs from the forecast to the dispatcher who knows the route. In either organisation, ask who can challenge a recommendation and what evidence comes back to them after they act. That is a question a board can put to its own operations team before buying another dashboard.
