What if the most valuable AI research output is an argument you can check?

Shanghai AI Laboratory describes mathematicians using Intern-S2 to develop a proof of a specific case of Ghys’s conjecture, including complex dimension three. The work has a public preprint and a Lean formalisation repository.

The limits matter: this is research-stage evidence, not an autonomous solution to the entire conjecture. Three formalised theorems retain an explicit classical mathematical assumption.

Why China? My reading is that combining model developers, mathematicians and verification specialists creates a useful research workflow. The advantage lies in how those capabilities work together.

For Western research leaders, the question is whether AI exploration produces results that survive scrutiny. Fluent explanations are easy to generate; checked claims and visible dependencies are more valuable.

Thinking futureback about this signal, consider testing one bounded research question with a verification method agreed in advance. Track human corrections, rejected arguments and unresolved assumptions alongside the result.
