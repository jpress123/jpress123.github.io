What could a film jury learn from the cut a director threw away?

In Xiamen, 20 teams selected from 287 applicants had 72 hours to make a film on an assigned theme. The Golden Rooster and Hundred Flowers Film Festival announced its recommendations on November 9 as part of its first AI film unit. The festival had asked teams to keep a record of their process throughout the challenge.

That brief gives a festival two things to work with. On screen, an audience sees the scene that survived. In the production record, a filmmaker can point to the discarded version and explain why a different performance, sound or edit served the story better. That is a conversation a polished reel rarely starts on its own.

Thinking futureback about this signal, what would a Western commissioner learn by paying directors to walk through one rejected scene before awarding the next contract? If only the finished reel counts, whose editorial judgement earns the credit, and whose contribution disappears?
