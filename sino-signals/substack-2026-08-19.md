Sino-Signals | Wednesday, August 19, 2026 — China Puts a Number on Generative Sameness

A platform now rejects AI characters that look 65 percent alike, and Beijing writes the one-person AI company into national industrial policy.

Two signals today, and they meet in the same place. One is a platform enforcing a measurable quality floor on generated content. The other is the state naming the solo AI operator as a firm type worth cultivating. Both are rule adjustments to a supply of content and companies that costs almost nothing to produce.

## Hongguo Rejects AI Drama Characters More Than 65 Percent Alike

Hongguo, ByteDance's free short-drama platform, published rules for AI drama character creation that took full effect at midnight on August 3, 2026, after a self-correction window running July 22 to August 2. The platform now runs an AI facial-similarity comparison system with a quantified standard. Inside a single series, leads and core supporting characters must carry distinct features, expressions, and posture, and changing only hairstyle and costume over one underlying face template counts as a violation. Across series, any character scoring above 65 percent facial similarity to an existing one is rejected. Urban romance, family warmth, and urban comeback titles fall under the standard, while fantasy, ancient-style, supernatural, and hand-drawn manhua dramas keep the old rules and live-action shot dramas sit outside it.

One decision reaches the whole supply chain, because Hongguo and Douyin sit between tens of thousands of studios and the audience, and AI titles already make up more than 95 percent of new micro-drama releases. The rules are benchmarked to the National Radio and Television Administration's grading and provenance requirements, so platform enforcement and state policy point the same way. Urban titles must lip-sync dubbing, vary camera language, label opening credits as AI-assisted production, and embed provenance metadata in the file.

This is the first platform rule anywhere that treats generative sameness as a measurable defect with a numeric threshold, and it turns a face into an asset that gets registered, compared, and defended. Copyright terms bite as hard. Training characters on unauthorized real-person photos, celebrity likenesses, or original illustrations is banned, and custom virtual likenesses need written authorization on file. Penalties escalate from rejection to takedown, revenue clawback, and account bans.

Monday morning action: ask whoever runs your generated-content pipeline what the reuse rate is on faces, voices, and character designs, and build the provenance record before a similarity threshold arrives in your market.

## China Writes the AI One Person Company Into National Industrial Policy

On June 18, 2026, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Cyberspace Administration of China, the National Development and Reform Commission, and four other departments issued a three-year action plan for the platform economy covering 2026 to 2028. The plan names the AI one person company, AIOPC, as a cultivation target, the first time the category appears in the task text of a national policy document. Li Qiangzhi of the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology places AIOPC at the base of a growth ladder running up through specialized small giants, manufacturing champions, gazelles, and unicorns. The plan also commits to three batches of platform opening lists and at least 100 resource-opening pilots by 2028.

Local governments moved first. More than 20 cities have published OPC-specific support policies, and Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Guangdong have written the category into their 15th Five-Year Plan documents. Wuxi's market regulator bundled policy processing, bank credit, and intellectual property support into one registration package for solo AI founders, who depend on exactly the platform compute and tooling the same document opens up.

Treating the solo operator as a formal industrial category, with registration, credit, and subsidy machinery attached, differs from celebrating solopreneurs in the abstract. It compresses the cost of forming a firm toward the cost of a software subscription. Chinese AI film creators report ten-minute shorts made for roughly 2,000 yuan in software against 150,000 to 200,000 yuan under traditional production.

Monday morning action: check whether your procurement and vendor-onboarding rules can transact with a single-person supplier, because most cannot, and ask what your smallest viable operating unit looks like if a team of twelve became a team of one with agents.

Both signals answer the same abundance. When content and companies both become nearly free to produce, the scarce things are distinction and provenance. China is building the measurement systems for both, in public, at market scale, and the thresholds it sets will travel.
