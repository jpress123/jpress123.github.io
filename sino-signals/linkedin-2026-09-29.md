What if a battery pack could warn you a month before it fails?

At the 2026 World Manufacturing Convention in Hefei, which opened September 20, Hefei Guoqi Intelligence showed its Qiwen Battery World Model. The company says it detects battery anomalies up to 30 days in advance and has more than 20 application cases across electric vehicles, two-wheelers, and energy storage. Hefei organized 140 companies and about 400 products at the event. The 30-day figure is the vendor's claim, and no independent validation has been published.

Why it matters in China: China builds most of the world's EV and storage batteries, so a fault-prediction model trained on fleet-scale cell data has a deployment base few Western suppliers can match. Local governments stage conventions that put vendors in front of factory buyers, which shortens the path from demo to pilot. Vertical models tied to one plant type, such as battery packs or waste incinerators, also avoid the cost of general-purpose systems.

Global impact for Western leaders: battery warranty and recall costs sit on the balance sheet of every automaker and storage integrator. If a Chinese model moves failure warning from days to weeks, it resets how safety and warranty risk are priced, and it will arrive bundled with Chinese cells and packs sold abroad. Western battery management software vendors face a competitor that learns from far more operating data.

Also today: China's National Health Commission cited a target of more than 100 digital-smart pharmaceutical factories by 2027, under a seven-department plan covering 2025 to 2030.

Monday morning action: ask your battery or fleet operations lead what lead time your current monitoring gives before a cell fault, and whether your supplier contract grants access to the underlying data. If a Chinese vendor offers predictive analytics with its packs, request a validation trial on your own failure history before accepting the vendor's figures.
