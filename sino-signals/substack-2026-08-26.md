# Sino-Signals | Wednesday, August 26, 2026 — Two Ways China Is Scaling Machines That Perform

*A streaming platform shipping AI-made feature films every two weeks, and a robot industry that now accounts for 97 percent of global humanoid shipments.*

Both of today's signals describe the same move at different scales. Take something that used to require assembling a large team, industrialize it, and ship faster than the incumbents can respond. One case is a film studio. The other is a factory floor.

## iQIYI Puts Three AI-Generated Feature Films on a Paying Streaming Service

On August 21, iQIYI and Changxin Media confirmed the Soul Ferryman "Fusheng Meng" trilogy. The first two films, Butterfly Dream and Celestial Maiden Dream, launched at 10am on August 22, exclusive to iQIYI, each running over an hour, each produced with generative AI. Guo Jingyu served as art director, Ding Kuan directed, Xiao Jixiangtian wrote, Li Zhichao produced. To restore the franchise's original trio at a younger age, the production licensed the actors' likenesses, had original cast members Yu Yi, Liu Zhiyang, and Xiao Yin record live voice performances, and used motion transfer to drive the on-screen performance. The third film follows. iQIYI moved from its first licensed AIGC feature, Qitan: Paper Blade Crossing the Ruins, to two more releases inside one month.

iQIYI can scale this because it owns demand and supply at once. It green-lights against its own subscriber data and distributes to hundreds of millions of accounts on day one. The Soul Ferryman universe already spans long-form drama, web film, a musical, and short drama, so the audience is built and the rights are pre-cleared. Chinese regulators opened a licensing route for AI-generated features this summer, settling compliance before production begins.

For Western media, the number that matters is cadence. A major platform is now releasing feature-length AI content roughly every two weeks, behind a paywall, using a rights structure that separates likeness, voice, and motion capture. That structure is a working template for a question American studios are still litigating.

Monday morning: identify catalog IP with no economic case for a new production, price an AI-assisted extension against the rights you would need to clear, and check whether your talent contracts treat likeness, voice, and motion capture as separable.

## China Ships 40,000 Humanoid Robots and Takes 97 Percent of the Global Market

Zhang Feng, party secretary of the China Institute of Electronics and chair of the China Humanoid Robot and Embodied Intelligence Council, released the 2026 Humanoid Robot Industry Development Report at the World Robot Conference in Beijing on August 20. Chinese makers shipped more than 40,000 humanoid robots in the first half of 2026, taking China's share of global shipments to 97 percent. AgiBot and Unitree hold the top two positions worldwide, ahead of Tesla, Figure AI, and Agility Robotics. The report describes a shift from small-batch builds to routine deployment, backed by a domestic supply chain running from chips and components to finished units.

The component base came from a decade of electric vehicle and drone manufacturing, so harmonic reducers, actuators, and battery packs are already domestic and cheap at volume. Central policy targets more than 100 high-value application scenarios and over 10,000 humanoids in commercial use by the end of 2027, giving manufacturers a demand floor while unit costs fall.

A 97 percent shipment share means the learning curve for embodied AI runs almost entirely through Chinese factories and Chinese deployment data. Operating hours produce training data for manipulation policies, and that compounds. Western industrial buyers face the position they already reached in solar and batteries: a thin domestic supply against a foreign one that is cheaper, more available, and improving faster.

Monday morning: map which vendors can deliver units at volume for any embodied automation on your roadmap past 2028, and cost one pilot cell against both a Chinese and a Western unit so the gap becomes a number.

## What Connects Them

Both signals are about throughput. iQIYI is not claiming its AI films beat conventional production, and the robot report does not claim Chinese humanoids are more capable than Tesla's. Each claims something harder to answer: more of them, faster, in market. When the argument shifts from capability to cadence, the response has to be industrial rather than technical.
