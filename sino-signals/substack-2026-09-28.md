## China Opens a Cold War Style AI Hotline With Washington

**What happened:** Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington concluded September 26 with the White House announcing a US-China Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue, a bilateral channel for AI incidents modeled on Cold War-era emergency hotlines. Vice-Premier He Lifeng and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent completed the first round of the underlying dialogue on September 20, during the eighth round of US-China trade talks in New York. China's Commerce Ministry confirmed it September 24, two days before the summit made it public. The next session is scheduled before November, bundled with a $30 billion tariff cut, tying AI safety diplomacy to trade relief for the first time.

**Why China can scale this:** Beijing negotiated from parity, not from behind. Xi told Trump the two nations share "the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good," language casting China as a co-equal steward of frontier AI. Wang Yi described the summit's purpose as putting relations "on equal footing." That framing matters because the dialogue grew out of friction points where China had been on the defensive, chip export restrictions and "distillation" accusations against Chinese labs, and moves that fight into head-of-state diplomacy, where Beijing has more room to shape the narrative.

**Global implications:** This is the first formal government-to-government AI incident channel between the two AI superpowers, unprecedented in scope if not yet substance. Neither side has specified what qualifies as an "SI incident." That ambiguity is itself a signal that Washington now treats Beijing as a dialogue partner on frontier AI safety, not solely a target for containment, a framing the EU, UK, and other AI Safety Institute members will watch closely.

**Monday morning action:** If your company runs a dual US-China AI strategy, treat this as evidence that export-control enforcement could ease incrementally, not as detente. Ask your government affairs team to flag the November session as the first real test of what this channel does.

## China Forces Financial Influencers Off Social Media Without a License

**What happened:** China's Financial Product Online Marketing Management Measures, issued by the People's Bank of China and seven other departments in April, take full effect September 30, this Wednesday. Ahead of the deadline, Douyin eliminated its "financial advisor" certification category, removing 6,888 accounts, and processed over 45,000 violations in the third quarter, blocking more than 1.5 million cross-border financial promotions and 30,000-plus illegal stock recommendations. Xiaohongshu published its first "Community Financial Ecology Covenant" on September 15 and has removed over 1.01 million violating notes and 157,000-plus accounts in six months.

**Why China can scale this:** Douyin and Xiaohongshu already run algorithmic content-classification systems built for earlier state campaigns, mandatory AI-content labeling, micro-drama review, so retrofitting them to flag unlicensed financial marketing took a policy trigger, not new engineering. The rule's sequence, licensed institution first, then credentials, then marketing, shifts financial content authority off influencers and onto regulated institutions, a reset only possible where centralized gatekeepers and real-name verification already exist.

**Global implications:** Western platforms face the same problem, unlicensed finfluencers and AI-cloned personas pushing stock tips that drive retail investors into scams, but rely on reactive takedowns after harm occurs. China's approach previews a different template, verify credentials before an account can discuss financial products at all, ahead of EU and US finfluencer-disclosure debates that don't yet require platform-level licensing checks.

**Monday morning action:** If your firm markets financial products into China through influencer or livestream partners, audit every account against the new credential requirements before September 30. Elsewhere, treat this as an early test case for platform-level licensing gates, worth revisiting once enforcement data is in.
