If your platform flags a writer’s work as AI-generated, what evidence can the writer actually challenge?

In a case presented by the Beijing Internet Court on November 10, a platform hid a user’s answer and barred them from posting for a day. The platform said its detector and a human reviewer had identified unlabelled AI content. When challenged, it cited technical secrecy. The court ordered it to restore the answer and delete the violation record.

One detail matters for anyone designing an appeals process. The user had written their answer on the spot. The court considered it unreasonable to expect a draft or source file, while the platform controlled the evidence behind its decision.

Thinking futureback about this signal, would your platform give its appeals team the power to reverse a detector’s verdict when nobody can explain it to the writer? If producing a draft becomes the price of being believed, whose spontaneous work disappears first?
