Sino-Signals | Tuesday, September 8, 2026 — Two Ways to Make AI Cheap Enough to Deploy

Both of today's signals cut cost out of the system rather than adding capability on top of it.

Western AI competition is largely a race on capability, measured in benchmarks and frontier releases. Two Chinese signals from the past week point somewhere else. One prices a capable agent model low enough that mid-sized firms can run agent workflows at all. The other removes a model from the robot entirely so that a factory can afford two hundred of them. Different layers, same instinct.

## iFlytek Ships an Agent-Grade Model Trained Entirely on Chinese Silicon

iFlytek released Spark X2.5 on September 7, a 293-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model activating roughly 30 billion parameters per inference, with a 256K context window and coverage of more than 200 languages. The company states the model was trained end to end on a fully domestic compute platform. It is live on iFlytek's Xingchen MaaS platform at 1.6 yuan per million input tokens, 0.24 yuan for cached input, and 6 yuan per million output tokens. On September 1 the company open-sourced two on-device versions, X2.5-4B and X2.5-1.7B, both with one-million-token context windows and both trained on the same domestic stack. The stated focus of the upgrade is code generation and agent orchestration.

The pricing is the scaling mechanism. At roughly 22 US cents per million input tokens, an agent workflow that fires thousands of calls to finish a single task becomes affordable to Chinese manufacturers and schools that would never license a frontier Western model. iFlytek already sells into tens of thousands of classrooms and appears on government procurement lists, where domestic-silicon provenance now clears rules that favor local compute.

Globally, this moves Chinese accelerators past the inference-only assumption that held through 2025, joining Meituan's trillion-parameter pre-training run on 50,000 domestic chips and Baidu's Kunlunxin training of Ernie 5.1. Western products built around agent loops now compete against a capable model at a fraction of frontier token cost, in 200 languages, with a permissive on-device tier beneath it.

Monday morning: price one real agent workflow against Spark X2.5 rates and against your current vendor, then check whether the 1.7B open-weight model runs your edge case on hardware you already ship.

## Tsinghua Spinout Cuts the World Model Out of Robot Deployment

Guangxiang Technology and Professor Li Shengbo's group at Tsinghua released Phi-WM 1.0 ActEffect, a world model that scores the physical consequences of candidate actions during training and is then removed from the robot at deployment. The policy generates three complete action proposals, the world model predicts each outcome in a frozen DINOv3 feature space, and a ranking loss pushes refined proposals toward the observed future. Reported results are 98.8 percent average success on LIBERO, 80.3 percent on the distribution-shifted LIBERO-PLUS against 51.5 percent for Fast-WAM, and 67.5 percent on RoboCasa-GR1 in a 29-dimensional action space, 9.2 points above the next system. Guangxiang, incubated in April 2025 by Tsinghua's School of Vehicle and Mobility and School of AI, ran its Phi-Bot X1 at a NIO welding load-and-unload station for three days, 21.5 cumulative operating hours, with no errors or interruptions.

Chinese factories buy robots by the fleet, so inference cost multiplies with every station. A world model that runs at training time and disappears at execution removes GPU, power, and maintenance overhead from every copy, which is the arithmetic that decides whether a pilot spreads from one welding cell to two hundred. The company raised over 100 million yuan in the first half of 2026 with IDG Capital leading, and sits inside an automotive supply chain that will validate or reject the approach within a production quarter.

Most Western embodied-AI programs are scaling world models up and running them online, betting that richer prediction at inference buys reliability. This work takes the opposite trade. If the real-machine numbers hold, cost per deployed arm diverges between the two architectures, and the cheaper one wins factory contracts regardless of which demos better. The caveat matters: these results are simulation-based, and the new model has yet to be validated on production hardware.

Monday morning: ask each embodied-AI vendor what runs on the robot at execution time versus only during training, then price that difference across the number of stations you plan to deploy.

Read together, the two signals describe a deployment doctrine rather than a research agenda. Capability is treated as a solved-enough input, and the contested variable is what it costs to run that capability ten thousand times a day across classrooms, welding cells, and edge devices. Western strategy meetings that benchmark models against models will keep missing this, because the competition is happening on the invoice.
