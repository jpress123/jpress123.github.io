When AI enters a car factory, who redesigns the work around it?

Dongfeng reports more than 500 AI application scenarios and over 1,500 agents online, alongside humanoid work on its Mengshi production line. These are company-reported deployments, not independently verified gains in productivity or safety.

The China signal is organisational: a vehicle group is putting AI into its operating environment. My reading is that the opportunity lies in connecting engineering knowledge with everyday factory decisions, while giving people clear responsibility for exceptions.

For Western mobility leaders, agent counts offer little guidance on their own. The useful questions concern the work: who checks a recommendation, who can override it, and whether operators spend less time correcting mistakes. Sustainability belongs in that assessment too, through material waste and rework rather than an assumed green benefit.

Thinking futureback about this signal, consider running a small, supervised trial around one production exception. Let operators help design the workflow. Track resolution time, rework, waste and human interventions before expanding.
