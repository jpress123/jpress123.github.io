# Sino-Signals | Friday, September 18, 2026 | Who Owns the Moment a Customer Stops Choosing

*Ant hands Starbucks a template for agent commerce, and thirty-five Chinese researchers publish the ladder for AI that improves itself.*

Two signals today sit at opposite ends of one question, which is how much deciding moves from people to systems, and who keeps the record when it does. One is a live consumer product with a global brand attached. The other is a paper that hands a whole research field its vocabulary.

## Ant's Agent Takes the Standing Coffee Order Starbucks Used to Own

On September 9, Ant Group launched a standing-order coffee service with Starbucks inside Ah Bao, the AI-native version of Alipay. A user gives one instruction and the agent places the same drink at a set time each morning. Ant answered questions from Inside China AI about how it works. The first time, the customer signs in to their Starbucks membership account through Starbucks' own mini-app inside Alipay. From then on, Ah Bao calls Starbucks' services over MCP, the open standard for connecting AI systems to outside services, and passes only the order details. No customer identity is sent to Ant, because Starbucks already holds it through the membership.

Alipay reaches close to a billion users, and Ant has assembled five phone makers and 16 carmakers around Ah Bao, so the instruction can start at a side button or a car dashboard. Alibaba's separate agent, Qwen, carries 167 million monthly users and routes shop, courier, map, and wallet through one conversation. No company in the US or Europe can assemble that chain today, and a buyer trying to acquire it would meet competition regulators.

The terms settle the split global brands have argued over. Starbucks keeps identity and purchase history, so the customer is still its customer. Ant takes the moment the order starts. Once a person has told an agent to order the same drink daily, a competitor stops competing to win a choice and starts competing to get the instruction changed.

Monday morning: map which agent platforms your customers already use, and ask what your brand receives back from an integration, not only what data leaves.

## China's Top Labs Chart Five Stages to AI That Rebuilds Itself

Thirty-five researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Tsinghua University, ByteDance, and the Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory posted "The Last AI Built by Humans" to arXiv on September 10 and revised it on September 15, filed as arXiv:2609.11873. The paper sets out five levels of recursive self-improvement, from a model executing upgrades humans designed, through diagnosing its own weak spots and choosing what it learns next, to redesigning the improvement process itself. It introduces a Headroom-Closed Index to measure how much capability current large language models have left, then classifies 491 existing papers on the ladder. It places 75.4 percent at levels one and two, and 5.9 percent at level five.

Automating the training and evaluation cycle converts researcher headcount into a compute problem, which suits a state that has committed 3.8 trillion yuan to domestic AI compute. Shanghai AI Lab, Tsinghua, and ByteDance already trade staff, datasets, and evaluation harnesses, so one published ladder gives rival groups a way to divide the work. Beijing's AI for Science plan, issued in August, already orders autonomous laboratories as the research default.

US firms hold the compute advantage here, so the Chinese move is to publish the taxonomy instead. A public ladder lets any laboratory state which level it has reached, and it hands regulators a vocabulary for autonomy claims before any Western agency has written one.

Monday morning: place your own AI programs on the ladder, then ask any vendor claiming autonomous improvement which of the five levels it operates at.

## The connection

Both signals write the rules of a market before it exists. Ant defined the split between merchant and agent by shipping it with a name-brand partner. The RSI authors defined the technical ladder by publishing it and sorting the field's work onto it. The definition arrived first. Standards bodies arrive later.
