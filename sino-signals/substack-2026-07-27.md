## China's AI Companion Rules Take Effect, Banning Virtual Partners for Minors

China's Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services took effect on July 15, 2026, the country's first national rules written specifically for AI companions and emotional chatbots. Five agencies issued the order, led by the Cyberspace Administration of China with the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security, and the market regulator. Providers must label every interaction as machine generated, remind users after two continuous hours, ban virtual partner and virtual relative services for minors, and intervene when a user signals self harm. Any app with 1 million registered or 100,000 monthly active users must file a security assessment with provincial regulators.

China can enforce this because app stores must verify a provider's filing before listing, which hands Beijing one choke point across the whole companion market. Penalties reach 200,000 yuan where a user's life or health is harmed, and the provider carries the liability.

Western companion apps such as Character.AI and Replika face mounting lawsuits over minors and emotional dependency, yet no US or EU rule targets the category directly. China now has an enforceable template that names the harms, caps continuous use, and assigns responsibility, and other regulators will borrow from it.

If you ship a conversational or companion feature, audit it against these rules this week and build in disclosure, usage reminders, minor detection, and a self harm escalation path before a regulator or plaintiff requires them.

## Ant Group Launches Enterprise Agent Factory to Set China's Deployment Standard

Ant Group's digital technology arm unveiled a Super Factory for Business Agents at the World AI Conference in Shanghai, running on an application platform called Agentar 2.0 that ships with nearly 200 job level digital expert templates and hundreds of subscribable agent tools. Vice President Sun Lei said the platform cuts enterprise agent delivery time by 72 percent and research and development cost by more than 65 percent while holding 99.9 percent operational stability. In finance it reports answer accuracy of 90 to 95 percent and 85 percent consistency with human specialists. Ant, Tencent, Alibaba, and Baidu all launched competing enterprise agent platforms at the same conference.

China can scale this because Ant built the stack on its own vertical models and more than twenty years of payments, insurance, and wealth management experience, then packaged it for resale to banks, hospitals, and energy firms. A single vendor can hand an enterprise a pre-tested agent that clears financial compliance out of the box.

Standardized, compliance ready agents sold as products reset the unit economics of enterprise AI, where US buyers still pay for bespoke integration. Ant is also drafting a China specific engineering standard it calls an industry Harness, and pay by result pricing would pressure every vendor selling agents by the seat.

Enterprise buyers should benchmark their current agent build cost against Ant's claimed 72 percent faster delivery, then ask vendors for outcome based pricing tied to accuracy and ROI.
