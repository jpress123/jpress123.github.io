Sino-Signals | Tuesday, August 11, 2026 — China's Models Win the Cloud While Its Robots Reach the Market

Two signals, one theme: China is now exporting AI as both software and hardware, and both moved this week.

For two years the default script held that US closed models owned the technical high ground and Chinese labs ran behind. The 2026 record is rewriting it. American enterprises are quietly rebasing their software on Chinese open-weight models, and Unitree is taking a profitable humanoid robot business public.

## US Firms Switch Their AI Base to Chinese Open-Weight Models

Over eight weeks to August 5, 2026, five Chinese labs shipped frontier models: Alibaba Qwen3.8-Max, Moonshot Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Zhipu GLM-5.2, and ByteDance Seedance 2.5. OpenRouter data shows the share of US enterprise tokens served by Chinese models rose from 4.5 percent in the first half of 2025 to more than 30 percent after February 2026, peaking near 46 percent, a tenfold jump in 18 months. Coinbase set Kimi and GLM as the default for all its engineers, DoorDash tested Kimi for support and basic coding, Airbnb has leaned on Qwen since October 2025, and the startup Lindy moved 100 percent of its traffic from Claude to DeepSeek-V4 and cut inference cost about 90 percent.

Why China can scale this: open-weight releases built on mixture-of-experts designs and heavy inference optimization let vendors tune, test, and ship a full high to low model matrix on a fast cycle while holding cost down. The Stanford 2026 AI Index puts the top US to China performance gap at 2.7 percent, so most business workloads no longer need the frontier.

Global implications: closed US labs recover training costs in the hundreds of millions through high API prices, the exact structure a 60 to 90 percent cheaper open-weight rival undercuts. One estimate puts the same workload at about 25 dollars on Claude and 0.18 dollars on DeepSeek, and US regulators are moving to restrict Chinese models even as US firms adopt them for cost.

Monday morning: audit AI spend and adopt tiered model selection, routing high-throughput, non-critical work to open-weight Chinese models and piloting a private deployment this quarter.

## Unitree Prices Its Humanoid Robot IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market

Unitree Robotics set its STAR Market listing price at 150.80 yuan per share on August 6, 2026, with online subscription on August 10 under ticker 688836. The Hangzhou company is floating 40.4 million shares, about 10 percent of its stock, to raise 4.2 billion yuan, with market estimates putting the listing valuation above 42 billion yuan, near 5.8 billion dollars. Revenue grew from 159 million yuan to 393 million yuan to 1.699 billion yuan across the last three full years and swung to a net profit of 278 million yuan. The review ran 73 days from filing to approval, the fastest this year.

Why China can scale this: Unitree builds quadruped robots and G1 and H1 humanoids on China's dense robotics supply chain, pricing hardware far below Western rivals and shipping in volume to developers, factories, and consumers. Founder Wang Xingxing has aligned the firm with the 15th Five-Year Plan, which puts embodied AI at the center of industrial policy, and a public listing hands a hardware maker patient domestic capital.

Global implications: a humanoid maker reaching public markets with real revenue and profit shows embodied AI in China has moved from demo to industry. Unitree already undercuts Boston Dynamics, Tesla Optimus, and Figure on unit price, and IPO proceeds give it a war chest to widen that lead.

Monday morning: benchmark humanoid and quadruped unit costs against Unitree's price list before scoping automation pilots, and track how the company spends its raise.
