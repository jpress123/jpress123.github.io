What happens to the economics of American AI when Silicon Valley starts running on Chinese models?

It is already happening. Over eight weeks to August 5, five Chinese labs shipped frontier models: Alibaba Qwen3.8-Max, Moonshot Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Zhipu GLM-5.2, and ByteDance Seedance 2.5. OpenRouter data shows the share of US enterprise tokens served by Chinese models rose from 4.5 percent in the first half of 2025 to more than 30 percent after February 2026, peaking near 46 percent. That is a tenfold jump in 18 months. Coinbase set Kimi and GLM as the default for all its engineers. DoorDash tested Kimi for support and basic coding. Airbnb has leaned on Qwen since October 2025. The startup Lindy moved 100 percent of its traffic from Claude to DeepSeek-V4 and cut inference cost about 90 percent.

Why China can scale this: open-weight releases built on mixture-of-experts designs and deep inference optimization let vendors tune, test, and ship a full model matrix on a fast cycle at low cost. The Stanford 2026 AI Index puts the top US to China performance gap at 2.7 percent, close enough that most business workloads no longer need the frontier.

Why it matters for Western leaders: closed US labs recover training costs in the hundreds of millions through high API prices, the exact structure a 60 to 90 percent cheaper open-weight rival undercuts. One estimate puts the same workload at about 25 dollars on Claude and 0.18 dollars on DeepSeek.

Monday morning: audit your AI spend and adopt tiered model selection. Route high-throughput, non-critical work to open-weight Chinese models, reserve frontier US models for the hardest tasks, and pilot a private deployment this quarter to measure cost and data-control gains.
