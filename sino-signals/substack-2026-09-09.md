Sino-Signals | Wednesday, September 9, 2026 — Beijing Buys Compute and Reprices Craft

Two ministry documents in one week, one aimed at silicon and one aimed at silk, both written to move China up a value chain.

MIIT published two documents worth reading together. One is a five-year blueprint for computing capacity built on domestic chips. The other is China's first national policy for its time-honored industries, where AI is assigned to decode craft knowledge that was never written down. One targets the cost floor of intelligence, the other the price ceiling of culture.

## China Commits 3.8 Trillion Yuan to Build AI Compute on Domestic Chips

MIIT posted its 15th Five-Year Plan for the information and communications sector on September 7, dated August 12 and covering 2026 to 2030. The plan sets 9,800 exaflops of intelligent computing capacity by 2030, against a 2025 baseline of 1,590 exaflops, plus 1,700 exabytes of advanced storage. Cumulative information infrastructure investment is set at 3.8 trillion yuan, about 532 billion dollars, with private capital expected to supply most of it. The document calls for orderly deployment of clusters running 100,000 or more accelerator cards and for greater efforts to adapt infrastructure to home-grown computing chips, across 13 headline indicators and 26 key tasks.

China can scale this because compute siting runs through state grid planning, provincial land allocation, and the East Data West Computing corridors on a single approval timetable. Domestic chipmakers now have a published demand curve to finance against, which converts a target into order books for Huawei, Cambricon, and their suppliers. In August, a Zhengzhou node activated the country's first 100,000-chip cluster built entirely from domestic silicon.

Globally, a capacity target sized for Chinese hardware shifts the binding constraint from chip access to domestic fabrication yield, which is a harder thing for Washington to reach. Western cloud providers should expect Chinese inference capacity to price aggressively into Southeast Asia, the Gulf, and Africa well before 2030. Read the number with care, though: the plan leaves the measurement method for intelligent computing capacity undefined.

Monday morning, model your inference economics against Chinese capacity clearing at half your rate in third markets, and track quarterly cluster activations in Zhengzhou, Shenzhen, and Hohhot as the honest progress metric.

## China Orders AI Into Its Time-Honored Crafts to Win Global Pricing Power

Also on September 7, MIIT and five other ministries issued China's first national policy document for silk, tea, porcelain, traditional Chinese medicine, brewing, arts and crafts, and the four treasures of the study. Vice Minister Xie Yuansheng set targets of 50 firms above 10 billion yuan in annual revenue and 100 consumer brands by 2028, moving toward world-class status by 2030. The document gives AI three assignments: digitize craft knowledge, decode traditional production processes, and lift factory-floor quality. Xie noted that AI-assisted design already generates thousands of silk weaving patterns in minutes. The first national exposition of time-honored industries opens in Hangzhou in late October.

The base is already there. These sectors run about 25,000 firms above designated size, employ nearly 3.5 million people, and turn over roughly 8 trillion yuan a year. China holds 45 UNESCO intangible heritage inscriptions and more than 100,000 listed items, with 200,000 practitioners trained through university programs over the past decade.

The stakes are explicit. China exports about 90 percent of its silk output as raw material or OEM goods, and MIIT's Feng Haicang named the recovery of pricing power as the objective. Generative design over a documented pattern archive compresses the design cycle that European luxury houses treat as a moat, and licensing deals this summer already placed hemp weaving, Huzhou brushes, and Zhuang brocade on 2026 FIFA World Cup products.

Monday morning, audit where your brand premium rests on design lead time and archive depth, then ask your Chinese craft suppliers whether they are moving from OEM contracts to their own labels.

Source: [Xinhua via China.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/2026-09/08/content_118684824.shtml)

Both documents describe the same move at different altitudes: own the input so you can set the price of the output. Compute is the input to intelligence, craft archives are the input to cultural premium, and Beijing has decided to fund the digitization of both. The test in each case is delivery, not target, so watch cluster activations in Zhengzhou and brand margins in Hangzhou.
